Every area in The Gambia, compared

Start from your budget or start from the map. Each area below has its own guide with prices, schools, access, what the money buys and what to check before you pay.

The Gambia is small enough to drive end to end in a day, and its property market still ranges from D13,333 per square metre of land in Fajara down to D71 in Gambissara. Distance to the Atlantic explains most of that. The rest is title, access and whether anyone else is building nearby. If you are buying a plot rather than a finished house, our overview of what land costs area by area sets out plot prices per area.

Under D1,000 / m²

A full compound plot upcountry, on the North Bank or in the southern coastal villages. Land here is cheap because almost nobody is buying it — and the thinner the local evidence, the wider the figure can be of a specific street.

D1,000 – D3,000 / m²

The working middle of the market: the Kombo highway belt, the inland villages being subdivided right now, and the fishing coast south of Tanji. This is where most Gambian families and most diaspora builders actually buy.

Kombo Coast

The Atlantic strip, and the reason most people arrive. Prices fall steadily as you go south: the same money buys a finished villa in Kololi or a beach plot with room to build in Kartong.

Kombo Inland

Where most Gambian families and most diaspora builders actually buy: the highway belt behind the coast, from Brusubi in the north down to the villages being subdivided along the Sukuta–Jambanjelly road. Ten minutes from the beach at the top of this list; three quarters of an hour at the bottom.

Remittance country. Gambissara in particular is built almost entirely with money sent home from abroad, and it is the cheapest land we publish anywhere in The Gambia.
