Land for sale in The Gambia

Bare land is what most people actually buy here. This page is the plot half of the MyKunda price index: every area we track where the thing for sale is a piece of ground rather than a finished house.

Of the 41 areas MyKunda tracks, fourteen are markets where the typical property advertised is a plot, not a villa. They run in a band from the coast at Brufut down to Kartong, and inland from Sukuta out to Yundum. The table gives what a standard 1000 m² plot is asked for in each one, alongside that area's average price per square metre and the year-on-year move.

The two price columns are not the same thing

Typical plot asking price is what a walled or unwalled plot of about 1000 m² is advertised at in that area — the figure to budget against. Avg. price / m² is the area's index across everything that sells there, built and unbuilt, so it sits far above what a square metre of bare ground costs. Use the first to price a plot; use the second only to compare one area with another.

They are asking prices, not sale prices

The Gambia has no public register of transaction prices, so nobody can publish verified sale prices — including us. What is published here is what sellers and agents ask, measured the same way across every area. Expect to negotiate, and expect the gap to depend on how long the plot has been on the market.

Before you pay for a plot

Price is the easy part. Two plots in the same street can differ by half on title alone, and land is where nearly every Gambian property dispute starts: the same plot sold twice, a family member selling what belongs to the whole family, customary land that has also been leased by the State to someone else.

So settle what kind of title is on offer before you settle the price — start with freehold, leasehold and customary land explained, and if you are serious about a specific plot, order an independent ownership check before any money moves.
