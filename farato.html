The vibe

Farato grew along the Brikama–Serrekunda highway, and it still reads that way: a ribbon of shops, workshops, fuel and transport either side of the road, with compounds spreading back behind it into what was farmland a generation ago. Gazetteers file it under its older name, Medina Suware Kunda. It is not a quiet village waiting to be discovered — it has a market, a secondary school, a police station and, since June 2026, the country's new National Emergency Treatment Centre.

That hospital is the single biggest thing to happen to land values here. It was opened by the president in June 2026, houses the national reference laboratory and diagnostic imaging centre, and is intended to grow into a 1,500-bed teaching hospital. Banjul International Airport is under three kilometres away. Farato is, in short, well placed — which is exactly why its land is fought over.

Farato has only 4 priced plot listings with a known size — below our bar of 5 for an area-specific rate. The rate above is the band rate for the inland Kombo villages between the Brikama highway and the Sukuta–Jambanjelly road: 13 priced plot listings pooled across Farato, Latriya, Mamuda and Jambanjelly. It tells you what the band asks, not what a specific street asks. The 4 listings we can price in Farato itself ask a median of D1,827 per m² — too few to call a market, shown here as context, not as a rate.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller. We have no rent listing we can verify here, so we quote no rental yield for this area either — a yield built on a rent we had to guess would only look like knowledge.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every reported sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.

What has happened to land at Farato

Farato has a documented, unresolved land dispute that has run for nine years, and any honest page about buying here has to lead with it. The root of it is an undemarcated village boundary between Farato and neighbouring Bafuloto, and the pattern repeats: a family head or alkalo of one village sells farmland the adjoining village also claims, an estate company subdivides and resells it, buyers build, a court finds for the original claimant, and eviction follows.

The milestones are on the record. In May 2017 a court-ordered demolition on land between Farato and Bafuloto ended in a riot, with machinery burned and the highway blocked. In 2019 the West Coast Region governor opened an investigation into the Farato land dispute after properties were demolished by the High Court Sheriff Division; the alkalo of Farato Bojang Kunda asked the government to demarcate the village boundary. In 2021 the High Court struck out an estate company's application and ordered purchasers to vacate. In April 2022 the alkalo of Bafuloto publicly warned that already-documented plots near the Bafuloto–Bojang Kunda border were being resold. In May 2024 the High Court Sheriff issued eviction notices to more than 950 property owners at Farato, most of whom had bought from one estate company believing the papers were good. In February 2025 a renewed demolition attempt was driven off by residents. The boundary is still not demarcated.

What that means in practice: at Farato the vendor's chain of title matters more than the plot. Establish which village's land the parcel is on, get the chain of title back beyond the person selling it to you, and have a lawyer search it before any money moves. Buying from an estate company is not a substitute for that search — the 950 owners had bought from one. Our guide to verifying land title explains the searches, and freehold, leasehold and customary land explains why a “freehold” sold by a family head may be neither.

Getting there and what is on the ground

Farato sits directly on the Brikama–Serrekunda highway, the main road between the coast and Brikama, with Banjul International Airport about 2.8 km to the north-east. A 5.6 km Farato–Jambur road linking the highway to the Sukuta–Jambanjelly road was begun in 2021; we could not confirm that it has been completed.

The village has a market, the MingDaw junior and senior secondary school, a police station and, since June 2026, the National Emergency Treatment Centre. Mains water reaches Farato but is rationed: residents interviewed in September 2025 described queuing before dawn for two gallons apiece. We found nothing reliable on grid electricity coverage, and no record of flooding in the village itself — only of the road becoming hard to use in the rains before the new road was begun.

What we have not measured here yet

Farato is one of five areas we added to MyKunda in August 2026, and its page is deliberately thinner than our older ones. Rather than fill it with plausible numbers, here is what we do not know.

  • Population — no census figure for Farato we could retrieve.
  • Rents — no rental listing we can verify, so no rent and no yield are published.
  • Grid electricity coverage — not established.
  • Whether the Farato–Jambur road was completed.
  • Any resolution of the 2024 eviction notices. As far as we can tell the matter is still open.

Farato is recorded in GeoNames under its older name, Medina Suware Kunda, at 13.315 N 16.665 W; the pin above is the village market as mapped in OpenStreetMap, 194 m from it. Note that three other places in The Gambia are also called Farato — in Upper River, North Bank and Central River. Mapping sites often return the Upper River one.

If you live here, sell here or have bought here, you can correct any of this: tell us what we have wrong. That is how the rest of these pages got better.
