Sending money to The Gambia — what it really costs

AC Awa Camara 15 August 2026 8 min read

On a property purchase, the transfer is not an afterthought — it is a line item worth thousands. The World Bank's own corridor data shows most of the cost of sending money to The Gambia is hidden in the exchange rate, not in the fee. Here is how to see it and how to cut it.

The fee is not the cost

The World Bank's Remittance Prices Worldwide database records, for each provider, both the advertised fee and the exchange-rate margin — the gap between the real market rate and the rate the firm gives you. On the UK-to-Gambia corridor, that margin is where the money goes.

A representative reading from that corridor: one internet provider charged a zero fee but applied a rate of D84.00 against a market reference of D91.58 — an exchange-rate margin of 8.28%, which was the entire total cost. Others charged a small fee of around £1–£2 alongside margins of 8.26 – 8.95%, giving total costs of roughly 9.8 – 9.9%.

A "no fee" transfer to The Gambia can be the most expensive one on the page. Compare the dalasi that arrive, never the fee that is charged.

For context: the global average cost of sending a remittance is 6.36%, and the Sub-Saharan Africa average sits around 8.5% — among the highest of any region. The Gambia corridor is at or above that regional average.

What that costs on a property purchase

The percentages look small until you apply them to a purchase price rather than to a family transfer.

Remittance pricing is measured on small transfers — typically £200 or $200 — and margins usually narrow on larger amounts. But the direction of the lesson holds: on a purchase-sized transfer, always negotiate the rate, and always ask what lands in the recipient account rather than what leaves yours.

Reference rates to check against

The Central Bank of The Gambia publishes daily valuation rates. In August 2026 they were roughly:

Any quote materially below these is charging you the difference. Check the mid-market rate on the day, not the rate you remember.

Rules for property money specifically

  • Pay from your own account into the named seller's or lawyer's account. Never a third party, never a different name, never "my brother will collect it".
  • Keep every transfer receipt. They are your proof of payment if the deed is ever disputed, and your evidence of funds if you are ever asked.
  • Match the transfer to the contract. The sums and the dates in your bank statements should reconcile line by line with the payment schedule in the sale agreement.
  • Fix the currency of the deal. An instalment plan quoted in dalasi is a multi-year bet on the exchange rate; the same plan fixed in euros is not. Ask for the total to be stated in hard currency in the contract.
  • Send in fewer, larger transfers where the contract allows. Every transfer pays a margin.
  • Never send money before the title checks are complete. A recovered transfer is a rare event.

Why this matters to the market

Diaspora money is not a side story in Gambian property — it is the market. The Central Bank recorded US$775.6 million of remittance inflows in 2024, an extraordinary figure for an economy of this size, and a substantial share of it goes into land and building rather than into consumption. Every percentage point shaved off the cost of sending it is money that arrives as blockwork instead of as somebody's margin.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to send money to The Gambia?

Compare the dalasi that arrive, not the fee that is charged. World Bank corridor data shows providers advertising a zero fee while applying an exchange-rate margin above 8% — which becomes the entire cost. For purchase-sized sums, a bank transfer with a negotiated rate is usually cheapest and creates the paper trail a lawyer needs.

How much does it cost to send money to The Gambia?

Total costs on the UK–Gambia corridor commonly run from about 8.3% to 9.9% of the amount sent on small transfers, mostly in the exchange-rate margin rather than the fee. The global average is 6.36% and the Sub-Saharan Africa average around 8.5%. Margins usually narrow on larger amounts if you negotiate.

What is the exchange rate for the Gambian dalasi?

The Central Bank of The Gambia publishes daily valuation rates. In August 2026 they were roughly D72.6 to the US dollar, D85.5 to the euro and D96.8 to the pound. Any quote materially below these is charging you the difference.

How should I pay for a property in The Gambia?

From your own account into the named seller’s or lawyer’s account — never a third party. Keep every receipt, make the transfers reconcile line by line with the payment schedule in the sale agreement, and never send money before the title checks are complete.
