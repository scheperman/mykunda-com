Residency, permits and retiring in The Gambia

AC Awa Camara 15 August 2026 9 min read

Buying a house in The Gambia does not give you the right to live in it. Property and immigration are entirely separate systems here, and the paperwork that lets you stay is annual, administrative and cheaper than most people expect. Here is how it works.

Owning is not residing

There is no residence-by-investment route in The Gambia. A registered deed in your name gives you a property right and nothing else — no visa, no permit, no entitlement to remain. You will hold your house on one legal footing and your presence in the country on another, and both need maintaining.

Getting in, and staying past the stamp

Visitors are generally admitted for a short period on arrival, and stays can be extended before they run out. British citizens, for example, do not need a visa in advance and receive a 28-day stamp on arrival, extendable twice by 28 days — up to about 84 days in total — at the Immigration Office in Banjul or at Tourist Police stations in the Tourism Development Areas. On some routes an initial period of up to 90 days applies.

Once those extensions are exhausted, staying legally means applying for an Alien ID card and a residence permit from the Gambia Immigration Department. There is no informal version of this step.

The permits, and what they cost

The Gambia Immigration Department issues a Residential Permit A (the right to stay) and a Residential Permit B (the right to work and stay), alongside an Alien Identity Card for non-ECOWAS residents aged 18 and over. Nationals of Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Sierra Leone and Ivory Coast are issued a Non-Gambian ID Card under bilateral arrangements instead.

At August 2026 rates (about D85.5 to the euro), a D5,000 permit is roughly €58 and a D2,500 alien card roughly €29. The expatriate quota tax is the one large number on the page — around D50,000 a year for non-ECOWAS nationals and roughly D10,000 for ECOWAS nationals — and it bites on employment, so it belongs in any salary negotiation rather than in a retiree's budget.

Two useful details. A residence permit runs for a calendar year, and the fee is the same whether you take it out in January or November — so timing the first application matters. And dependants are included in the holder's permit: spouse and children under 18.

Nationals of Mauritania, Guinea, Mali and Senegal are exempt by law from paying for a residence permit. Since July 2025, nationals of Nigeria and Guinea-Conakry pay a fixed D750, set under a ministerial directive applying the principle of reciprocity.

How the application actually runs

  1. Forms and payment are handled at the issuing centre through the contracted commercial bank, which issues the forms and the receipts.
  2. Documents: a signed passport valid at least six months with two blank pages; evidence of financial support for your stay (and, where you are working, a letter of responsibility from your Gambian employer with business references); and proof of vaccination as required for entry.
  3. Authentication of the form by a Justice of the Peace appointed by the Ministry of Justice.
  4. Interview with an approving officer appointed by the Director General.
  5. Issue of the permit and, for non-ECOWAS nationals, the Alien Card.

Applications can be lodged at the Gambia Immigration Department in Banjul or through a Gambian embassy before travel.

Permanent residence and citizenship

Permanent residence is not an automatic right and not a purchase. It is generally reached after several years of continuous legal residence — commonly cited as five to ten years depending on circumstances — with the usual conditions attached. ECOWAS citizens have privileged routes to long-term residence that non-ECOWAS nationals do not. Plan on renewing annually and treat permanent status as a possibility rather than a plan.

Retiring here: the practical picture

  • Budget the recurring paperwork, not just the purchase. A permit and an alien card each year is a modest but permanent line.
  • Healthcare is the real question. Serious treatment often means Dakar or Europe. Retain international health cover with medical evacuation; do not rely on being able to buy it after a diagnosis.
  • Income is taxed where the rules say, not where you feel resident. If you will let out a property, rental income tax is charged on gross rent at 8% residential or 15% commercial — see renting out property in The Gambia.
  • Currency risk runs both ways. A pension in euros against costs in dalasi has been comfortable in recent years, but the Central Bank's policy rate of 14% and inflation of 7.0% in April 2026 tell you this is not a stable-price economy.
  • Test a wet season first. Almost everyone visits between November and April. Living here in August is a different country.
  • Sort out succession early. A Gambian property held by a foreign owner without a will is a problem for your family, not for you — see inheritance and wills for Gambian property.

Frequently asked questions

Does buying property in The Gambia give me residency?

No. There is no residence-by-investment route in The Gambia. A registered deed gives you a property right and nothing more — residence is a separate annual permit from the Gambia Immigration Department.

How long can I stay in The Gambia without a residence permit?

It depends on nationality. British citizens receive a 28-day stamp on arrival, extendable twice by 28 days for about 84 days in total; on some routes an initial period of up to 90 days applies. After that you must apply for an Alien ID card and a residence permit.

How much does a Gambian residence permit cost?

Following the January 2024 revision, a non-ECOWAS Permit B is D5,000, ECOWAS Permit A is D3,000, ECOWAS Permit B is D2,500 and the Alien ID card is D2,500 — roughly €29 to €58 at 2026 rates. A permit runs for a calendar year at the same fee whenever it is taken out, and dependants (spouse and children under 18) are included.

Can I retire to The Gambia?

Yes, on an annually renewed residence permit rather than a dedicated retirement visa. Budget for the recurring paperwork, keep international health cover with medical evacuation since serious treatment often means Dakar or Europe, and make a Gambian will for your Gambian property.
