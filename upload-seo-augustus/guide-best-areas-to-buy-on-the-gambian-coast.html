The best areas to buy on the Gambian coast

AC Awa Camara 15 August 2026 9 min read

The Kombo coast packs a lot of variety into a short stretch — from the buzz of Senegambia to quiet southern beaches. Here's how the main areas compare, and who each one suits.

Kololi & Senegambia

The lively heart of the coast: beaches, restaurants, hotels and nightlife along the famous Senegambia strip. Prices are among the highest, and demand from holiday-let investors is strong. Best for rental income and an easy, sociable lifestyle.

Cape Point & Bakau

Established, leafy and a little more refined, with sea-view homes and a settled expat community. A good choice for a comfortable coastal base close to the city.

Brufut & Bijilo

Newer gated estates and villas, slightly further from the strip, with good value for space. Popular with families and buyers who want a quieter base within easy reach of the action.

Sanyang, Tanji & Kartong

The southern beaches: more natural, more space, lower prices, and where much of the beachfront land is. Best for buyers with a longer horizon — building a villa, an eco-lodge, or holding land as it appreciates.

What the coast costs, area by area

The clearest way to compare areas is the price of bare land per square metre, because it strips out the age, size and finish of whatever is standing on it. These are indicative averages for undeveloped plots — road access, services and title type move any individual plot up or down.

The spread is the story: land in Kololi costs roughly ten times land in Kartong, over a drive of about an hour. What you are paying for at the top of that range is proximity to the tourist strip and the rental demand that comes with it; what you get at the bottom is space, beach and time.

What is driving demand

Two things, mostly. Tourism — 233,113 visitors arrived in 2025, and the coastal strip from Bakau to Brufut is where nearly all of them stay, which is what makes short-let yields on the strip work. And the diaspora — the Central Bank recorded US$775.6 million of remittances in 2024, a very large share of a small economy, and a substantial part of it goes into land and building rather than consumption. Growth of around 5.3 – 5.7% is projected for 2026 with construction among the leading sectors, which you can see for yourself in the number of half-built compounds along the coast road.

One planning point worth knowing: much of the beachfront belongs to the designated Tourism Development Area, where State land is administered with tourism development in mind and the Gambia Tourism Board has a say in what happens on it. If a beachfront plot seems unusually cheap, ask specifically what it may be used for and who has to approve it.
