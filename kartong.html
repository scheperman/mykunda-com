The vibe

Kartong is The Gambia’s southernmost village, sitting where the Allahein River meets the Atlantic at the Senegalese border. It’s a place of extraordinary natural beauty — river estuaries, mangrove forests, wide empty beaches and the Kartong Bird Observatory attract nature lovers and eco-tourists. A handful of eco-lodges (like Sandele and Boboi) have put Kartong on the sustainable tourism map. Land here is the cheapest on the entire coast, making it a frontier play for patient investors who want maximum space for minimum outlay.

Kartong attracts buyers with an eco-tourism plan rather than a house plan, and that is a letting business. See how to verify a land title and renting out property.

Evidence behind the land rate: band rate — 21 listings across the band. Behind the rent: no rental listings we can verify. Gambian tenants think in months; agents advertise by the year, and landlords normally ask six to twelve months up front, so both units are shown.

Kartong has only 2 priced plot listings with a known size — below our bar of 5 for an area-specific rate. The rate above is the band rate for the Sanyang-Gunjur coast: 21 priced plot listings pooled across Sanyang and Gunjur. It tells you what the band asks, not what a specific street asks. The 2 listings we can price in Kartong itself ask a median of D742 per m² — too few to call a market, shown here as context, not as a rate.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller. We have no rent listing we can verify here, so we quote no rental yield for this area either — a yield built on a rent we had to guess would only look like knowledge.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every verified sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.

Land: 95 priced plot listings across The Gambia, from Facebook Marketplace (25 August 2026), Songhai Properties, AccessGambia, Holprop and GamRealty. Houses and rents: 72 and 45 local listings. How we measure prices.
