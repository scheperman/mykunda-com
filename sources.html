How a source earns its weight

Every row of evidence carries a weight between 0 and 1, and the medians are weighted medians. A closed deal we witnessed counts 1.00; a notary figure 0.95; our own asking price 0.90; an agency's own list 0.85; a Gambian portal 0.60–0.65; an international aggregator 0.40, because that stock is wide but often years stale. Multiply that by how confident the parser was about the row itself, and a half-read listing can never outvote a real deal.

The same villa on four portals is one villa. Rows are fingerprinted on market, type, area, size to the nearest 25 m² and price to about 5% — anything matching keeps only the copy from the most trusted source, and anything matching one of our own listings loses outright. That number is on the counter above; if it ever collapses to nothing, a portal has changed its markup and the fingerprints stopped landing.

Harvesting is polite and internal. Every run reads robots.txt first and skips what it disallows, identifies itself, and waits between requests. Sourcing stays in the backoffice — no attribution is published on the public site, and no external listing is ever shown as ours.

When a portal redesigns, the parse breaks. That is the normal failure here, and it is meant to be loud: the source goes red, the log says what happened, and Test shows you the blocks it could not read so the pattern can be fixed in the console without a redeploy.
