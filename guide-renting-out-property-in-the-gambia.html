Renting out property in The Gambia — season, yields and tax

MK MyKunda Updated 31 August 2026 18 min read

A house on the Kombo coast can earn. It can also stand empty for half the year, because Gambian tourism is one of the most seasonal markets in West Africa. This guide sets out the demand pattern, the rent law that most landlord advice leaves out, the tax you owe on gross rent, and how to build a yield figure you can defend.

In short

  • The peak season is November to April — six months, on the Government's own tourism strategic plan. The widely repeated "90% in winter, 25% in summer" is hotel occupancy from a US Commercial Service country guide last published on 30 August 2022, and it is not evidence about a private apartment.
  • Long lets are governed by the Rent Act 2014, as amended by the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024: increases reportedly capped at 5% every three years, three months' written notice of an increase, and 90 days' notice before eviction. The Act text is not published anywhere we can reach, and enforcement is acknowledged to be weak.
  • Rental income tax is charged on gross rent, not profit — 8% residential, 15% commercial, no deductions, residents and non-residents alike (Gambia Revenue Authority).
  • Ministries, agencies, local authorities, diplomatic missions, international organisations and large taxpayers must withhold that tax at source. Those are exactly the tenants a long let on the coast is usually aimed at.
  • The yield table below is MyKunda's own working arithmetic, not market data: no Gambian body publishes rents, nightly rates, occupancy or management fees. On our assumptions a €80,000 two-bedroom nets €2,800–€3,600 (3.5–4.5%) as a short let and about €3,700 (4.6%) as a long let — before Area Council rates and ground rent, which nobody publishes either.

The season is everything

The Gambian tourist year runs on the European winter. Visitors arrive between October and April, and the Government's own Tourism, Culture and Hospitality Strategic Plan puts the peak at November to April. That is six months, not five. Recent season openings were 16 October 2023, 11 October 2024 and 4 October 2025.

Two consequences follow, and they should shape how you buy and how you budget:

  • Annual yield figures built from peak-season nightly rates are close to meaningless. Multiply the nightly rate by realistic annual occupancy, not by 365 and not by the peak.
  • A long-term let to a resident tenant earns less per night and much more per year — and it comes with a statute attached, which a holiday let does not obviously have. Which model wins depends on the property, the area and how much management you are willing to buy.
What we could not verify

The figure you will see everywhere — "average hotel occupancy reaches around 90% in the winter months and barely 25% for the rest of the year" — comes from a single source: the US Commercial Service Gambia Country Commercial Guide, last published on 30 August 2022, written in the COVID recovery when the same page recorded a 41% fall in arrivals in 2021. No second reliable source states it, and the Gambia Bureau of Statistics publishes no occupancy series we could reach. It is also hotel occupancy in a charter and package market, where beds are filled by tour operator contracts. A self-catering apartment competes for a different and smaller slice of that demand. Use the figure as dated background, not as an input to your own numbers.

On arrivals: the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture reported 233,113 arrivals in 2025, up 3% on 2024, in figures presented to the National Economic Council and reported by Kerr Fatou in March 2026. Set that against 235,800 in 2019, recorded in the World Bank's 2022 project appraisal for the tourism sector: six years on, arrivals are recovering towards the pre-pandemic level rather than past it. The Ministry of Finance's 2026 budget sets a target of 298,042 for 2026, which is an aspiration, not a measurement.

The established source markets are the UK, the Netherlands, Germany, Sweden, Denmark and Norway. The World Bank records that 65–80% of arrivals came from ten European countries, with around 35% from the UK or the Netherlands alone. The 2024/25 season line-up reported in the Gambian press added Belgium, Portugal and the Baltic states, plus regional and Turkish routes. We could find no reliable source naming Italy or Poland as Gambian source markets, so we no longer say so.

The law a long let sits under: the Rent Act 2014, as amended

If you let residentially on a long tenancy, you are not free to set your own terms. The operative statute is the Rent Act 2014, as amended by the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024. The Rent (Amendment) Bill 2023, a private member's bill sponsored by Hon. Madi M. K. Ceesay, passed its consideration stage on 15 March 2024, was passed by the National Assembly on 21 March 2024, and is recorded as "Assented" on the National Assembly's bill tracker, retrieved 31 August 2026.

What Gambian parliamentary reporting says the amended Act does:

What we could not verify

We could not obtain the text of the Rent Act 2014 or of the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024. No official Gambian gazette or consolidated-law repository we could reach publishes either. Every provision in the table above rests on parliamentary reporting by Foroyaa, Kerr Fatou, The Standard, The Alkamba Times and The Point, not on the statute — so treat the detail as indicative and have a Gambian lawyer read the Act before you rely on a clause. Enforcement is also weak on the public record: Hon. Fatoumatta Jawara told the National Assembly in August 2024 that "we have approved the Rent Act; however, enforcement is still a challenge", and Foroyaa reported in July 2025 that the law was failing to protect tenants from practices it was meant to end. A law that is poorly enforced still binds you if your tenant takes you to a tribunal.

Separately, the Ministry of Justice has published a page for a validation workshop on a Rent and Tenancy Bill 2025. That page is the only trace of it we could find, it is blocked to automated retrieval, and no such bill appears on the National Assembly's bill tracker as at 31 August 2026. We therefore say only that it is reported to exist. We do not describe what is in it.

A holiday let of a few nights is a different animal, and the Act's tenancy machinery is not obviously designed for it. That is our reading, not a published position — see deposits and licensing below.

Tax: on gross rent, not on profit

This catches almost every first-time landlord. Rental income tax is charged on the rent you receive, with no deductions allowed. Your cleaner, your manager, your platform commission and your generator diesel do not reduce the taxable figure. Non-residents are taxed on Gambian rental income in the same way as residents — being abroad is not an exemption. It is a final tax on that income.

When you pay. Rental income tax is paid quarterly, by the 15th of the month after the quarter ends, with an annual return by 31 March. Registering for a TIN with GRA is the practical first step. Capital gains tax on a sale is the seller's tax, the return is due within 15 days of the disposal, and it must be paid before the deed can be registered — which makes it a completion risk for your buyer as much as a cost for you. What we could not verify

Two things. First, how GRA treats a self-catering apartment or a villa let by the week. The only boundary GRA publishes is negative — hotels, guest houses, inns and boarding houses are not "residential" — and the Ministry of Finance's 2025 budget separately announced a forthcoming "system to regulate and tax short-term rentals", which is evidence the category was still being worked out, not that it is settled. Earlier versions of this guide said the 15% commercial rate "typically applies" to short lets. We should not have: it is a reasonable expectation, not a published rule. Ask GRA in writing and keep the answer.

Second, GRA's own two answers on contract withholding. The current GRA withholding tax brochure and the 2025 budget speech give 10% / 5% / 8% as above. GRA's domestic taxes FAQ page still shows an older "10% for Gambian residents and 15% for non-residents". We publish the brochure and budget figures as the current position and flag the conflict rather than hiding it. Confirm the rate with GRA before you sign a renovation contract.

What changed: GRA has been building digital rental-tax systems since 2024, and The Point reported in January 2026 that the first phase of a Rental Compliance System was rolled out in October 2025. That is one outlet's sentence in a review of GRA's year, so we attribute it rather than state it flatly — but the direction is not in doubt, and the Ministry of Finance's 2026 budget counts on "rolling out of the rental income solution". Rental income that used to pass unnoticed increasingly does not. Register, declare and keep receipts: a retrospective assessment is a far worse outcome than the tax itself.

When your tenant deducts the tax before paying you

This is the change most likely to surprise a diaspora landlord, and it lands precisely on the tenants a coastal long let is usually marketed to. Since the 2025 budget measures, an institutional tenant must withhold the rental income tax at source and pay it to GRA. In the words of the 2025 budget speech:

A tenant, who is a Ministry, Government department or agency, local authority, diplomatic mission, international organization, established entities or a person under the category of large taxpayer occupying a property used for commercial or residential purposes shall at the time of making payments withhold tax on the rental income at the rate of 15% in the case of a property used for commercial purpose, and 8% in the case of a property used for residential purposes.

What that means in practice:

  • An embassy, a UN agency, an NGO with international-organisation status or a ministry will pay you net. If you have budgeted the gross rent, your income is 8% lower than you planned on the day it arrives.
  • An ordinary private tenant does not withhold. The obligation to declare and pay stays with you, quarterly.
  • Get the withholding certificate or receipt every time. It is your evidence that the tax on that rent has been paid. Rental income tax is a final tax, so withholding at the right rate should discharge it — confirm that with GRA for your own facts rather than assuming it.
  • Say in the tenancy agreement whether the rent is gross or net of withholding. A clause that quietly requires the tenant to gross you up is a clause an institutional tenant may refuse; better to price it in.

The lesson is not that short lets are bad. It is that the headline gross of a short let flatters it, that the quiet long-term tenant is more competitive than most owners expect once tax and management are counted, and that neither model earns anything at all if the property is not actually managed. Note too that the long-let figure comes with the Rent Act's 5%-every-three-years ceiling attached, so a rent set low is a rent you live with.

Where a euro figure has to be converted, we use the Central Bank of The Gambia's official exchange rates for valuation purposes — D85.60 to the euro on 21 August 2026. The site header carries the live rate.

The recurring costs a yield table usually omits

It is often said that The Gambia has no property tax. That is not right, and for a landlord it matters twice a year.

  • Area Council rates. The Local Government Revenue Initiative records that the eight local governments are empowered by the General Rate Act 1992 to levy rates on property, valued under the Rating Valuation Act 1987, with valuation rolls often long out of date. Councils are pushing collection: Kerr Fatou reported in June 2026 that Brikama Area Council approved incentive payments to Alkalolu and Seyfolu to improve property-rate collection. One reported figure — a Brusubi Phase 1 rate rising from D400 to D2,000 — comes from an opinion piece in The Point in 2020 and is one resident's account, not a published schedule.
  • Annual ground rent on a State lease. If you hold a state leasehold, ground rent is payable to the Department of Lands, historically before 31 January, and non-payment exposes the lease to re-entry. The last published basis is Schedule V of the State Lands Regulations 1995. The Ministry of Finance's 2026 budget commits to strengthening compliance on annual land rent, so this is being enforced more, not less.
What we could not verify

We could not obtain the text of the General Rate Act 1992, and no council, ministry or GRA publication we could reach publishes a current rate schedule or a current ground-rent schedule. So we cannot tell you what either will cost on a given property, and we do not quote the 1995 ground-rent slabs as if they were current. An earlier version of this page said that unpaid rates attach to the property rather than to the person who ran them up. We could not source that, so we have removed it as a statement of law — ask the Area Council for a written statement of account for the property, and ask the seller for rate receipts, on the footing that arrears may be pursued against the property.

Deposits, agents and whether a short let needs a licence

Deposits and advances. The evidence conflicts. Foroyaa reported in July 2025 that the 2024 amendment "limits the requirement to pay an advance payment to only one month", while noting that asking for six months or more continues in practice; Foroyaa's earlier account of the bill as introduced, in December 2023, described a three-month limit. Because the Act text is unpublished we cannot resolve it. What is certain is the surrounding vacuum: there is no tenancy-deposit protection scheme in The Gambia and no statutory client-money rule for agents, so a deposit held by an agent is protected by your contract and nothing else. Name the sum, the holder, the account, the deductions permitted and the return deadline in the tenancy agreement.

Agents and managers. There is no licensing regime and no professional register for Gambian estate and letting agents, and no published commission benchmark or statutory cap, so there is no fallback rule if your contract is silent about the fee or about who pays it. Agents are reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2012, supervised by the Financial Intelligence Unit, and consumers can complain to the Gambia Competition and Consumer Protection Commission — so "no regulator at all" is wrong, but there is no licensing regulator. A Real Estate Agents Bill was validated at a stakeholder workshop in April 2026; it has not been introduced in the National Assembly and it is not law. Our guide to estate agent regulation sets out what protection actually exists.

Licensing a short let. Not established either way. The Gambia Tourism Board plainly controls land inside the Tourism Development Area — the Ministry of Tourism instructed GTB in January 2025 to place a temporary hold on all new TDA land applications pending an audit, and that suspension was still the position when we checked. Whether a private apartment or villa let by the week must hold a GTB licence is a different question, and we could not answer it: the Gambia Tourism Board Act 2011 text is not published anywhere we could reach, and neither GTB's nor the Ministry's public sites carry licensing or registration pages. The honest advice is to ask GTB in writing before you list, and keep the reply.

Consent to let at all. On a state lease, regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 says the lessee shall not "subdivide, convey, assign or otherwise alienate the premises or any part thereof by sale, transfer of possession, lease or sublease without the consent of the Minister in writing first obtained". On its face that language reaches a lease or sublease, not only a sale. We have not found a published statement of how the Department of Lands applies it to an ordinary residential tenancy, and we are not going to guess. Read your own lease, and ask your lawyer whether letting your property needs the Minister's consent — see tenure explained.

Running it from abroad

  • Appoint a manager with a written contract stating the commission, what it covers, how often you get an account, who holds the keys, and what happens to money the manager collects. No Gambian rule requires an agent to keep your money in a separate client account, so say where it sits.
  • Put the tenancy in writing and have it stamped. Stamp duty on a tenancy or lease agreement is 2% of the amount, and an unstamped instrument is inadmissible in evidence — which is exactly when you would need it.
  • Register with GRA and diarise the dates. Quarterly by the 15th after quarter end, annual return by 31 March. If your tenant is an institution, collect the withholding certificates.
  • Keep a caretaker for a property that stands empty in the wet season. Salt air, humidity and an unoccupied house are a bad combination.
  • Budget for power and water backup. A holiday guest forgives a lot; they do not forgive no water.
  • Check your title allows letting. Covenants and conditions attach to a State lease, and the consent question above is unresolved. Inside the Tourism Development Area, ask GTB before you list.
  • Move rent through a licensed bank. See sending money to The Gambia for what that costs and what the rules are.

Before you buy to let: what to check

If you are buying specifically for rental income, the diligence is wider than an ordinary purchase. Work through this before you commit, alongside how to verify a title.

  1. Verify the title first. A rental property with a defective title is a defective property that happens to have a tenant. Ask which of the Gambian documents the seller actually holds — lease, certificate of occupancy, deed of conveyance, Alkalo transfer — and what each one proves.
  2. Read the lease covenants and the permitted use, and take advice on whether letting needs the Minister's written consent.
  3. Ask whether the plot is inside the Tourism Development Area, and if so what GTB's current position is. New TDA land applications have been suspended since January 2025.
  4. If there is a sitting tenant, you are buying the tenancy too. Get the written agreement, the rent history, the date of the last increase and the notice given for it. Under the Rent Act as amended you may be three years away from your next permitted increase, and it may be capped at 5%.
  5. Ask for the GRA rental-tax receipts for the letting history, not just the tenancy agreement. Historic non-declaration is far more visible now than it was.
  6. Ask for the Area Council rate receipts and, on a state lease, the ground-rent receipts. Get a written statement of account from the Council rather than a verbal assurance.
  7. Ask for twelve months of booking or tenancy records, not a good month and not a screenshot.
  8. Price the management before you price the rent. On our own arithmetic, management and cleaning are the largest single deduction on a short let — larger than the tax.
  9. Confirm the water supply, the power supply and what happens when each fails, and walk the route from the property to the beach or the road at night rather than at noon.
  10. Test the seller's yield claim against your own table. If a quoted yield only works at peak-season nightly rates and peak-season occupancy, it is not a yield.

If you are buying from abroad, read buying as a foreigner first: buying property confers no residence right, and a non-Gambian's position on land is not the same as a Gambian's.

If you already own it: a landlord's checklist

  • Register for a TIN and start declaring. Quarterly by the 15th after quarter end; annual return by 31 March; tax on gross rent, no deductions.
  • Write the tenancy down and stamp it. Include the rent, the term, the deposit and who holds it, repairs, subletting, notice, and whether the rent is gross or net of any withholding.
  • Diarise your increase window. On the reported cap you can raise the rent by no more than 5% and no more often than every 36 months, with three months' written notice. Set the rent correctly at the start rather than planning to correct it later.
  • Do not use self-help on a non-paying tenant. The reported notice period before eviction is 90 days, and the Rent Act's route is the rent tribunal. Changing the locks is not a remedy.
  • Keep the premises habitable. The amended Act is reported to place that duty on the landlord.
  • Do not demand rent in dollars or euros. The amendment was reported as aimed squarely at that practice.
  • Know where to complain, and where your tenant can complain about you — rent tribunals for tenancy disputes, the GCCPC for consumer matters, the FIU for money-laundering concerns about an agent, the police for fraud, and the civil courts for breach of contract. Gambian property litigation is reported to run over three years, so the contract is worth more than the remedy.
  • Plan for what happens if you die owning it. A tenancy survives the landlord and the estate inherits the tax position. See inheritance and wills.

Questions people actually ask

How much rental income tax do I pay in The Gambia?

Eight per cent of gross rent on residential lettings and 15% on commercial, published by the Gambia Revenue Authority. It is charged on gross rent, not profit, so management fees, cleaning, commission and utilities do not reduce the taxable amount, and non-residents are taxed in the same way as residents. It is a final tax on that income.

Is a holiday let taxed at 8% or 15%?

Not established. GRA defines the boundary only negatively: residential property "does not include hotels, guest houses, inns, boarding houses or similar establishments". A self-catering apartment or a villa let by the week is not classified anywhere GRA publishes, and the Ministry of Finance announced a forthcoming system to regulate and tax short-term rentals in the 2025 budget. Ask GRA in writing and keep the answer.

When do I pay, and what do I file?

Rental income tax is paid quarterly, by the 15th of the month after the quarter ends, with an annual return by 31 March. Register for a TIN with GRA first. Capital gains tax on a sale is a separate return, due within 15 days of the disposal.

Will my tenant deduct the tax before paying me?

If your tenant is a ministry, a government department or agency, a local authority, a diplomatic mission, an international organisation or a large taxpayer, yes — they must withhold 8% on residential and 15% on commercial rent at source and pay it to GRA. An ordinary private tenant does not withhold, and you remain responsible for declaring and paying. Collect the withholding certificate every time.

How much can I put the rent up by?

Gambian parliamentary reporting on the Rent Act 2014 as amended by the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024 describes a cap of 5% in every three years, with a minimum interval of 36 months and at least three months' written notice of any increase. We could not obtain the Act text, so have a Gambian lawyer confirm the clause before you rely on it.

How much notice do I have to give before eviction?

Ninety days in writing, on the reporting of the 2024 amendment, extended from the shorter period that applied before — and then through the rent tribunal, not by self-help. Enforcement of the Act is acknowledged to be weak in practice, but that cuts both ways: it does not make the notice period optional for you.

How much deposit or advance rent can I ask for?

The evidence conflicts. Foroyaa reported in July 2025 that the amendment limits an advance payment to one month, while noting that six months or more is still commonly demanded; its earlier account of the bill as introduced described three months. There is no tenancy-deposit protection scheme and no statutory client-money rule for agents, so whatever you agree needs to be written down, with the holder and the return conditions named.

What is the tourist season in The Gambia?

Arrivals run from October to April, with the peak from November to April — six months, on the Government's tourism strategic plan. Recent seasons opened on 16 October 2023, 11 October 2024 and 4 October 2025. The often-quoted 90% winter and 25% summer occupancy figures are hotel occupancy from a US Commercial Service country guide last published on 30 August 2022, and they are not a guide to a private apartment.

What rental yield can I expect on a Gambian property?

Nobody publishes a Gambian yield series, so no honest answer exists. On MyKunda's own working assumptions for an €80,000 two-bedroom near the strip — set out in full on this page — a short let nets about €2,800 to €3,600, or 3.5% to 4.5%, and a long let about €3,700, or 4.6%, before Area Council rates and ground rent. Those are our assumptions, not market data. Replace them with your own quotes.

Is there really no property tax in The Gambia?

No. Area Councils levy rates on developed property under the General Rate Act 1992, valued under the Rating Valuation Act 1987, and a state leaseholder also owes annual ground rent to the Department of Lands. Neither has a current published schedule we could obtain, so ask the Council and the Department for the figures on your property.

Does a short let need a licence from the Gambia Tourism Board?

Not established. GTB clearly controls land inside the Tourism Development Area, and all new TDA land applications have been suspended since January 2025. Whether a private short let needs a GTB licence is not published — the Gambia Tourism Board Act 2011 text is not available anywhere we could reach. Ask GTB before you list, and keep the reply in writing.

Do I need to charge VAT on rent?

The rental or supply of residential property is exempt from VAT, so a long residential let is outside it. VAT is 15% and registration becomes compulsory once taxable supplies reach D2,000,000 in a tax year — roughly €23,000 at the Central Bank's valuation rate of D85.60 to the euro on 21 August 2026. A short-let operation treated as commercial and turning over more than that is a live VAT question, and GRA's VAT brochure does not address guest accommodation expressly. Take advice.

Can I price the rent in euros or dollars?

Be careful. The 2024 amendment was reported as specifically aimed at landlords demanding rent in foreign currency, notably US dollars. The euro figures on this page are for a foreign reader's convenience; take Gambian advice before writing a foreign currency into a tenancy agreement.

Am I allowed to let my property at all?

Read your lease. Regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 requires the Minister's written consent before a lessee may "convey, assign or otherwise alienate the premises … by sale, transfer of possession, lease or sublease", and on its face that language reaches a letting. We could not find a published statement of how the Department of Lands applies it to an ordinary tenancy. Ask your lawyer rather than assume.
