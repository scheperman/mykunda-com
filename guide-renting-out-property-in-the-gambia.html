Renting out property in The Gambia — season, yields and tax

MK MyKunda Updated 15 August 2026 10 min read

A house on the Kombo coast can earn. It can also sit empty for six months a year, because Gambian tourism is one of the most seasonal markets in West Africa. This guide sets out the demand pattern, the tax you actually owe, and how to work out a yield you can believe.

In short

  • Average hotel occupancy reaches around 90% in the winter months and barely 25% for the rest of the year, so a holiday let earns most of its annual income in roughly five months.
  • Rental income tax is charged on gross rent, not profit, at 8% residential and 15% commercial, and non-residents are taxed exactly as residents are.
  • On a worked €80,000 two-bedroom near the strip, a short let nets about €3,000–€3,600 (3.8–4.5%) after tax and management, while a long-term let at €450 a month nets about €3,700 (4.6%).
  • The Gambia Revenue Authority began rolling out the first phase of a Rental Compliance System in October 2025, so letting income that once went unnoticed increasingly does not.

The season is everything

The Gambian tourist year runs on the European winter. The bulk of visitors arrive between October and April, with November to April the peak, and the difference between the seasons is not marginal — average hotel occupancy reaches around 90% in the winter months and barely 25% for the rest of the year.

That single fact should shape everything about how you buy and how you budget:

  • A holiday let earns most of its annual income in roughly five months.
  • Annual yield figures quoted from peak-season nightly rates are close to meaningless — multiply the nightly rate by realistic annual occupancy, not by 365.
  • A long-term let to a resident tenant earns less per night and much more per year. Which model wins depends entirely on the property and the area.

The demand itself is real and growing: 233,113 visitors arrived in 2025, and the 2024/25 season was projected at over 232,000. The traditional source markets are the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark and Norway, with Italy, Belgium and Poland more recently added.

Tax: on gross rent, not on profit

This catches almost every first-time landlord.

ItemRate
Rental income tax — residential8% of gross rent
Rental income tax — commercial (includes most short-let and business use)15% of gross rent
Company-held property27% corporation tax, or a turnover-based minimum
Capital gains when you sellHigher of 15% of the net gain or 5% of the selling price
Withholding on contracts (repairs, renovation)10% resident, 15% non-resident

Gross means gross. Your cleaner, your manager, your platform commission and your generator diesel do not reduce the taxable figure. Non-residents are taxed on Gambian rental income in the same way as residents — being abroad is not an exemption.

There is no general annual property tax, but Area Councils levy rates on developed property, and unpaid rates attach to the property rather than to the person who ran them up. If you buy a house that has been let for years, ask for the rates receipts.

What changed: the Gambia Revenue Authority began rolling out the first phase of a Rental Compliance System in October 2025. Rental income that used to pass unnoticed increasingly does not. Register, declare and keep receipts — retrospective assessments are a far worse outcome than the tax itself.

The lesson is not that short-lets are bad — it is that the headline gross of a short-let flatters it, and the quiet long-term tenant is more competitive than most owners expect once tax and management are counted. And neither model earns anything at all if the property is not actually managed.

Running it from abroad

  • Appoint a manager with a written contract stating the commission, what it covers, how often you get an account, and who holds the keys.
  • Keep a caretaker for a property that stands empty in the wet season. Salt air, humidity and an unoccupied house are a bad combination.
  • Budget for power and water backup. A holiday guest forgives a lot; they do not forgive no water.
  • Insist on a signed tenancy agreement, even for a friend of the family — particularly for a friend of the family.
  • Check your title allows letting. On a State lease, covenants and conditions attach to the lease; on a plot inside the designated Tourism Development Area, the Gambia Tourism Board has a say in what a property may be used for. Ask before you list.

Before you buy for rental

  1. Ask for twelve months of booking or tenancy records, not a good month.
  2. Walk the route from the property to the beach or the road at night, not at noon.
  3. Confirm the water supply, the power supply and what happens when each fails.
  4. Price the management before you price the rent.
  5. Verify the title. A rental property with a defective title is a defective property that happens to have a tenant.

Yield table is illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast. Last checked August 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How much rental income tax do I pay in The Gambia?

Rental income tax is 8% on residential lettings and 15% on commercial — which typically includes short-let and holiday use. It is charged on gross rent, not on profit, so management fees, cleaning and utilities do not reduce the taxable amount. Non-residents are taxed on Gambian rental income in the same way as residents.

What is the tourist season in The Gambia?

The bulk of visitors arrive during the European winter, October to April, with November to April the peak. Average hotel occupancy reaches around 90% in the winter months and barely 25% for the rest of the year, so a holiday let earns most of its annual income in roughly five months.

What rental yield can I expect on a Gambian property?

Once realistic annual occupancy, tax and management are counted, net yields on a coastal apartment typically land in the region of 4 to 5% for both short-let and long-term models. Gross figures quoted from peak-season nightly rates overstate short-let returns substantially.

Do I have to register rental income with the Gambia Revenue Authority?

Yes. The GRA began rolling out the first phase of a Rental Compliance System in October 2025, so letting income is tracked far more closely than it used to be. Register, declare and keep receipts — a retrospective assessment is far worse than the tax itself.
