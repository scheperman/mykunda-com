The vibe

Mamuda is an inland Kombo South village, a few kilometres north of Jambanjelly and well back from the coast. The 2013 census records it — spelled Mahmuda — with 1,558 residents, which makes it a substantial place rather than a hamlet; it fields a team in the Kombo South district football tournament and was large enough to host a national party rally in January 2026. What it does not have is a place on the map: no gazetteer records a Mamuda in the Kombos at all.

What brings buyers here is price. Plots at Mamuda are advertised in phases, fenced and unfenced, at figures well below anything on the coast, and the listings we can measure are the cheapest per square metre of any Kombo area we track. It is a farming village being converted into residential plots, one layout at a time, and it is early.

Evidence behind the land rate: band rate — 13 listings across the band. Behind the rent: no rental listings we can verify. Gambian tenants think in months; agents advertise by the year, and landlords normally ask six to twelve months up front, so both units are shown.

Mamuda has only 4 priced plot listings with a known size — below our bar of 5 for an area-specific rate. The rate above is the band rate for the inland Kombo villages between the Brikama highway and the Sukuta–Jambanjelly road: 13 priced plot listings pooled across Farato, Latriya, Mamuda and Jambanjelly. It tells you what the band asks, not what a specific street asks. The 4 listings we can price in Mamuda itself ask a median of D948 per m² — too few to call a market, shown here as context, not as a rate.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller. We have no rent listing we can verify here, so we quote no rental yield for this area either — a yield built on a rent we had to guess would only look like knowledge.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every verified sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.

Land: 95 priced plot listings across The Gambia, from Facebook Marketplace (25 August 2026), Songhai Properties, AccessGambia, Holprop and GamRealty. Houses and rents: 72 and 45 local listings. How we measure prices.

What to know before buying at Mamuda

We found no documented land dispute at Mamuda itself, and we would rather say that plainly than imply either safety or risk. What we did find is next door: at Pacholling, roughly two kilometres west, an eviction operation in February 2024 destroyed more than 150 cashew trees, and a resident quoted in the reporting said the paperwork produced by the claimant indicated Mamuda — that is, the dispute turns partly on where Mamuda's land ends and Pacholling's begins. A separate Pacholling land protest was reported in April 2026. Mamuda is not a party to either; it appears as a boundary reference.

That is exactly the pattern to guard against in an inland village being subdivided: the plot is real, the seller is real, and the boundary between two villages' land is not written down anywhere. Get the chain of title in writing, establish which village's land the parcel sits on, and have a lawyer search it. Our guide to verifying land title sets out how, and freehold, leasehold and customary land explained covers what a family head can and cannot sell.

Getting there and what is on the ground

Mamuda lies roughly 1.5 km north of the Sukuta–Jambanjelly road, the 13.5 km tarred route inaugurated in 2020 that runs from Sukuta through Jabang, Jambur and Latriya to Jambanjelly. Beyond that we can establish very little: no school, health post, mosque, mains water or grid connection at Mamuda appears in any official register, press report or mapping dataset we could reach. A village market is mapped in OpenStreetMap by a single contributor. We are not going to fill that in with guesses.

What we have not measured here yet

Mamuda is one of five areas we added to MyKunda in August 2026, and its page is deliberately thinner than our older ones. Rather than fill it with plausible numbers, here is what we do not know.

  • School, health post, mains water and electricity — nothing we could source. Treat all four as unknown, not as absent.
  • Rents — no rental listing we can verify.
  • House prices — no local house listing we can verify; the figure above is land plus build cost.
  • An exact position. The village is in no gazetteer; the pin is accurate to roughly 2 km.
  • Any layout scheme or planning approval covering the plots being sold.

Mamuda is in no gazetteer — not GeoNames, not OpenStreetMap, not the OCHA settlement layer. It is in the 2013 census, spelled Mahmuda. The pin above is a village market point mapped in OpenStreetMap and is accurate to roughly 2 km. Do not confuse it with the Mahmuda in Upper Niumi, North Bank Region, which is what most mapping services return.

If you live here, sell here or have bought here, you can correct any of this: tell us what we have wrong. That is how the rest of these pages got better.
