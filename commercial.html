Before you sign

What a business needs to know first

Four things decide whether a unit works for your trade, and all four are asked of the person listing it — not left for you to discover after the deposit.

Fit-out level

Shell means bare walls and a floor, and the fit-out is yours. Fitted means finished but empty. Turnkey means you can trade from day one. The difference is months of work and a large part of your opening budget.

Power, and what happens when it goes

Whether there is three-phase power, and whether a generator or solar backs it up. A freezer, a workshop or a server room does not survive on the grid alone.

The service charge, separately from the rent

Listings state the service charge and how often it is billed, so a low rent with a heavy charge cannot be mistaken for a cheap unit.

Term, and when it comes free

The minimum term and the availability date are on the listing. So is the frontage to the street, which for a shop is worth more than the floor area behind it.

Worth knowing

Commercial letting is taxed differently

Rental income tax in The Gambia is charged on gross rent, not on profit: 8% on residential lettings and 15% on commercial, which typically includes short-let and business use. Transfer tax on a purchase is commonly around 5% of the declared value, with stamp duty on top. Whichever side of the table you are on, budget for it before you agree a figure. Rates are from the Gambia Revenue Authority, last checked August 2026.

Bank transfers clear once the banks release them. MyKunda only collects listing and service fees — we never take deposits, down payments or purchase money. Those belong in your lawyer’s client account — insist on that in writing, because no Gambian rule compels it.
