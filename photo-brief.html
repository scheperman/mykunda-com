Photo collection brief

Real photos for your Gambia listings

This brief lists every photo the MyKunda site needs, what to shoot and where, and how to source images you have the rights to use. Drop each photo onto its matching slot on the site — fill it once and it appears everywhere.

1 Where to get the photos

In order of quality and authenticity. The first two give you images you fully own and that are genuinely in The Gambia — always the best outcome.

Best

Your own listing photos

Photos taken at the actual properties by you or the selling agent.

  • 100% authentic & rights-cleared
  • Matches the real listing
  • Use a phone in landscape or a basic camera
Recommended

Local photographer

Commission a Kombo-based photographer / drone operator for a half-day shoot.

  • Drone shots of coast & plots
  • Consistent, professional look
  • Ask for full commercial usage rights in writing
Free libraries

Stock with a license

For area/atmosphere shots (beaches, river, markets) where a specific listing isn't needed.

  • Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay
  • Wikimedia Commons (check CC terms)
  • See the search terms in section 4

2 Technical specs

Keep things simple and consistent. Shoot in landscape unless noted, and compress before uploading (use squoosh.app or tinypng.com) so pages stay fast.

Format
JPG (or PNG). Aim for under 500 KB per image after compression.
Hero / area
Landscape, ≥ 1600 px wide. Wide coastal / beach scenes.
Listing & gallery
Landscape 4:3, ≥ 1200 px wide. 5–8 photos per property.
Agent portrait
Square, ≥ 400 px. Friendly headshot, plain background.

5 A few rules of thumb

  • Shoot landscape and hold steady — wide shots crop better into the site's slots.
  • Good light wins. Early morning and late afternoon flatter both buildings and beaches.
  • Get people's consent before using recognisable faces (especially the agent portrait).
  • Don't pull images from Google Images or other sites — most are copyrighted. Stick to the sources above or your own photos.
  • Keep a simple record — for each photo note where it came from and its licence, in case you're ever asked.
Note: I'm not a lawyer and licensing can be nuanced — when in doubt, use photos you took yourself or commissioned with written rights. That keeps you on the safe side and your listings genuinely Gambian.
