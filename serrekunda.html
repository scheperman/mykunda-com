The vibe

Serrekunda is The Gambia's largest city — a sprawling, vibrant metropolis of roughly 400,000 people that functions as the country's true commercial capital. The legendary Serrekunda Market is one of West Africa's great bazaars: a labyrinth of stalls selling everything from Dutch wax fabrics to freshly ground café Touba. Surrounding it is a dense urban fabric of compounds, mosques, small businesses and busy junctions. Traffic is hectic, the energy is relentless, and the cost of living is significantly lower than the tourist-facing coastal strip just a few kilometres west. For buyers and investors, Serrekunda offers the best value-for-money in the Greater Banjul Area — you're minutes from Kololi and the beaches, but at half the price per square metre.

Serrekunda is the one market where local buyers outnumber foreign ones by a wide margin, and local finance sets the pace. See bank mortgages and the real cost of buying.

Serrekunda has no priced plot listings with a known size — below our bar of 5 for an area-specific rate. The rate above is the band rate for the Kombo belt around Serrekunda: 31 priced plot listings pooled across Sukuta, Lamin, Jabang and Yundum. It tells you what the band asks, not what a specific street asks.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller. We have no rent listing we can verify here, so we quote no rental yield for this area either — a yield built on a rent we had to guess would only look like knowledge.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every verified sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.

Land: 95 priced plot listings across The Gambia, from Facebook Marketplace (25 August 2026), Songhai Properties, AccessGambia, Holprop and GamRealty. Houses and rents: 72 and 45 local listings. How we measure prices.
