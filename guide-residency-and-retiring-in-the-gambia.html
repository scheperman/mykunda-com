Residency, permits and retiring in The Gambia

MK MyKunda Updated 31 August 2026 16 min read

Buying a house in The Gambia gives you no right to live in it. Property and immigration are separate systems here, and the immigration side is annually renewed, discretionary and — this is the uncomfortable part — largely unpublished. This guide sets out what the entry rules actually are for your nationality, which permit a retiree needs, what a British pension does when you move, and where the official record simply runs out.

In short

  • Owning property gives you no residence right. There is no residence-by-investment route, no golden visa and no retirement visa. A retiree uses the ordinary, annually renewed residence permit.
  • Entry rules go by nationality, not by route or airline. British citizens get a 28-day stamp and two 28-day extensions; Americans need a visa (US$100–105 cash on arrival, plus a US$20 security fee each way); Dutch nationals have 90 days visa-free; French nationals get a free 15-day visa on arrival, renewable.
  • There are three permit types, not two: Type A, residence only, issued to retirees and students; Type B, residence and employment; Type C, employment, for unskilled workers.
  • The Gambia Immigration Department publishes no fee schedule we could reach on 31 August 2026, and the Type A fee — the retiree's permit — is not published anywhere we can trace. We have removed the fee figures we previously carried that we could not source.
  • Naturalisation needs 15 years of continuous ordinary residence and renunciation of your other citizenship; a Gambian citizen's spouse can apply to register after 7 years. Since January 2024 the fee is D75,000 non-ECOWAS, D25,000 ECOWAS.
  • For a British retiree the largest financial fact on this page is not a permit fee: the UK State Pension is frozen in The Gambia and never uprated.

Owning is not residing

A registered deed in your name gives you a property right and nothing else — no visa, no permit, no entitlement to remain. You will hold your house on one legal footing and your presence in the country on another, and both need maintaining, separately, for as long as you are here.

This is worth stating firmly because it is the single claim most often blurred in marketing. We looked for a residence-by-investment route and could not find one. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office states plainly that you need a residence permit for longer stays and describes no property exception (7 July 2026). The US State Department's entry section describes only visas and extensions (29 January 2026). The Gambia Investment and Export Promotion Agency — the national body that would advertise an investor visa if one existed — publishes nothing on investor residence at all. The most detailed Gambian explainer of the permit system lists three permit categories, all tied to residence, employment or study; property is not a category.

Nor does buying give you a shortcut into the land system itself. Schedule II of the State Lands Regulations 1995 provides that a non-Gambian shall not be allocated a plot of state land, and every assignment of an existing state lease needs the Minister's written consent — see buying property in The Gambia as a foreigner for what that means in a transaction.

Getting in: the rule is your nationality, not your route

We previously wrote that "on some routes an initial period of up to 90 days applies". That was wrong in a way that matters: the 90 days is a nationality rule, not an airline or itinerary rule. Each government publishes the position for its own citizens, and the positions differ sharply. The table below carries only what a government source says, with the date it said it.

Three costs at the border that are easy to miss

  • A security fee of €20 / US$20 / D1,000 per person, in cash, on arrival and again on departure. It is charged both ways, so budget it twice per trip.
  • Overstaying costs US$15 per month, payable in cash when you leave (US State Department, 29 January 2026). It is cheap enough to be tempting and it is still a record of non-compliance against your name.
  • Passport validity. The FCDO says only that your passport must be valid for the duration of your stay; the Dutch and Australian governments both require six months' validity on arrival. Carry six months and the conflict never arises.

On vaccination, the FCDO says a yellow fever certificate is required if you are arriving from a country listed as a transmission risk; Australia's advice states a valid certificate is required. Take the stricter reading.

Note what that table does not tell you. The D3,100 to D5,000 row is the Type C permit — the unskilled-worker permit — and we previously published it as the general non-ECOWAS work permit. A reader quoting that price at the counter for a retirement permit would be quoting the wrong permit and the wrong fee. There is no published price for a non-ECOWAS Type A permit. Until the Immigration Department publishes one, no honest budget for retiring here can include a permit line; ask the Department and get the figure in writing.

What we could not verify

The Gambia Immigration Department's own website was unreachable throughout our checks on 31 August 2026: gid.gov.gm fails TLS certificate verification and immigration.gov.gm returns a 502. There is therefore no reachable official fee schedule and no official statement of the application procedure, and nothing on this page attributed to the Department can be reproduced from the Department itself.

Because of that, we have deleted the following figures and claims, which this guide previously carried and which we could not trace to any reliable source: the expatriate quota tax (D40,000 rising to D50,000 a year for non-ECOWAS nationals, and "roughly D10,000" for ECOWAS nationals); an ECOWAS Permit A fee of D1,850 rising to D3,000; a machine-readable passport fee of D1,000 rising to D3,000; a D1,000 extension fee; the "previously D3,000" figures for the entry visa and entry clearance; the whole application procedure, including payment through a contracted commercial bank, authentication by a Justice of the Peace and an interview with an approving officer appointed by the Director General; the claim that a permit runs for a calendar year at the same fee whenever it is taken out — and the timing advice built on it; and the claim that dependants, meaning a spouse and children under 18, are covered by the holder's permit. None of these is disproved. All of them are unsourced, and several were load-bearing. Confirm current fees, the permit period and dependant cover with the Immigration Department itself before you rely on any of them.

Exemptions, and one contradiction we cannot resolve

FactCheck Gambia reported in August 2024 that citizens of Mauritania, the Republic of Guinea, Mali and Senegal are exempted by law from paying for a residence permit. Separately, Foroyaa reported on 18 July 2025 that the Ministry of the Interior issued a directive on 14 July 2025, on the principle of reciprocity, fixing the residence permit fee for Nigerian and Guinean nationals at D750, effective immediately, with an internal memorandum to all Regional Immigration Commands. The Department's public relations officer confirmed the figure publicly.

Those two statements sit awkwardly together: Guinea appears in both, once as exempt and once as charged. The likelier reading is that the July 2025 reciprocity directive supersedes the earlier exemption for Guinea, but no source we located says so, and we will not assert it. If you are Guinean, treat the position as unresolved and ask at the counter.

Applying: what is documented, and what is not

We have removed the step-by-step procedure this page used to carry, because none of its steps appears in any source we can reach. What can be said, with attribution, is short.

Where. Applications are lodged at the Gambia Immigration Department in Banjul, or through a Gambian embassy before travel. Extensions of a visitor stamp are dealt with at the Immigration Office in Banjul or, for British visitors, the tourist police stations in the Tourism Development Area.

What to take. FactCheck Gambia lists a passport valid for at least six months with two blank pages, a completed application form, a recent photograph, a criminal record check, a letter from an employer accepting responsibility together with evidence of financial support, and vaccination documentation. Two of those — the police check and the photograph — were missing from our previous list, and the police check is the item most likely to delay you, because it has to be obtained from your home country before you start. Note that FactCheck attributes this document list not to the Immigration Department but to a private migration consultancy, so treat it as a preparation checklist rather than as the official requirement.

What is not published. No monetary income threshold. No refusal criteria. No appeal route. No renewal criteria beyond the fact that permits are renewed annually. Nobody should tell you that renewal is automatic, because nothing published says it is.

Long stays, naturalisation and citizenship

This page used to say that permanent residence is "commonly reached after five to ten years". That was not sourced, and we could not establish that a Gambian permanent-residence status exists at all. It has been deleted. The real thresholds are in the Constitution and they are much longer.

  • Naturalisation — 15 years. Section 12 of the Constitution of The Gambia 1996 (revised 2018) requires an applicant to have been ordinarily resident in The Gambia for a continuous period of not less than fifteen years, to meet character requirements, to renounce any other citizenship and to take an oath of allegiance. The renunciation is the part people miss: naturalising means giving up the passport you have.
  • Registration as the spouse of a Gambian citizen — 7 years. Section 11 allows a person married to a Gambian citizen who has been ordinarily resident for not less than seven years to apply for registration.
  • Dual citizenship. Section 12A preserves Gambian citizenship for a Gambian who acquires another nationality. It does not waive the renunciation requirement that section 12 imposes on a foreigner naturalising.
  • Fees. Since January 2024, naturalisation and registration cost D75,000 for non-ECOWAS applicants and D25,000 for ECOWAS applicants — a fivefold increase on the previous D15,000 and D5,000.

ECOWAS citizens do have better routes, but narrower ones than is usually implied. The 1979 Protocol operates in three phases: entry without a visa for 90 days, then a right of residence, then a right of establishment. The IOM describes the first two as largely achieved and the third as still largely unfulfilled.

The practical planning point for a retiree: assume you will renew a permit every year for the rest of your life, and treat any settled status as a possibility rather than a plan. That has consequences for your will — see inheritance and wills for Gambian property.

Your pension, your tax, and the treaty

For a British retiree this section contains a larger number than any permit fee in this guide, and it is the number that compounds.

The UK State Pension is frozen in The Gambia

The UK State Pension is increased each year only for people living in the European Economic Area, Gibraltar, Switzerland, or a country that has a social security agreement with the UK allowing for uprating. The Gambia is not one of them. Your pension is still paid; it is simply frozen at the rate in payment when you first qualify or move, and it stays there. Over a twenty-year retirement the gap between a frozen and an uprated pension dwarfs every fee on this page. GOV.UK also notes that if you return to live in the UK, the rate goes back up to the current level. Get this into your spreadsheet before you get the permit fees into it.

There is a UK–Gambia double taxation treaty

The 1980 Gambia–UK Double Taxation Convention is in force, effective from 5 July 1982 — applying from 1 January 1980 in The Gambia and 6 April 1980 in the UK for income tax and capital gains. If your tax position is anything but trivial, the treaty text is the document your accountant needs; it is published by HMRC.

Gambian tax residency, and whether your pension is taxed here

What we could not verify

We cannot tell you how many days in The Gambia make you Gambian tax-resident, and we will not guess. The governing statute is the Income and Value Added Tax Act 2012 — named by the Gambia Revenue Authority on its own Domestic Taxes page — but the Act's text is not published by the GRA and is not among the statutes hosted on its downloads page, and the Ministry of Justice hosts no consolidated statute library. No reliable source we located states a 183-day test for The Gambia. Do not assume one applies.

For the same reason we take no position on whether a foreign pension is taxable in The Gambia. The GRA's published FAQs confirm only that residents earning employment income outside the country are liable to employment income tax under the foreign source rule, and that non-resident companies, partnerships and trusts are taxed only on Gambian-sourced income. Pensions are not addressed anywhere we could find. If a pension is your main income, write to the GRA and get the answer on letterhead before you move — and take advice on the UK side as well.

The Gambian taxes that are published

  • Rental income tax, if you let the property: 8% of gross residential rent, 15% of gross commercial rent, with no deductions allowed, and it applies to residents and non-residents alike. See renting out property in The Gambia.
  • Capital gains tax when you eventually sell, and it is the seller who pays: for an individual, the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration; for a company, partnership or trustee, the higher of 25% of the gain or 10% of the consideration. It falls due before the deed can be registered, and the GRA gives a 15-day return deadline. A retirement that ends in a sale, or in an estate that has to sell, meets this tax. The detail is in the cost of buying property in The Gambia.
  • Stamp duty on the purchase itself: 2% of the amount, paid by the buyer, under the Stamp Act as amended in 2019.

Retiring here: the practical picture

  • Driving. You can use a UK photocard driving licence in The Gambia for up to three months. After that you need a Gambian licence, applied for at a police station with proof of residency, your valid licence and a copy of your passport (FCDO, 7 July 2026). This is one of the few resident formalities with a clear published rule — do not let it lapse into month four.
  • Healthcare is the real question. The FCDO describes medical facilities as very limited, notes that private clinics will only treat fee-paying patients, and warns that the cost of medical evacuation can be very high. Hold international health cover that includes evacuation, and buy it before a diagnosis rather than after one. We previously said serious treatment "often means Dakar"; that specific claim is not in any source we located, so we no longer make it — what is sourced is that evacuation is expensive and local capacity is limited.
  • The law on same-sex relationships. Stated factually because anyone considering moving needs to know it: same-sex relationships are illegal in The Gambia and, in the FCDO's words, could lead to lengthy prison sentences. The FCDO also records zero tolerance of illegal drugs, and advises respecting local customs, particularly during Ramadan.
  • It is a cash economy. The FCDO notes that ATMs are unreliable outside tourist areas. Note also that the AML/CFT Act 2012 requires a declaration of US$7,500 or more in cash or bearer instruments on arrival and on departure, while the GRA's customs guidance states US$10,000 — treat US$7,500 as the safe threshold. See sending money to The Gambia.
  • Currency risk runs both ways. A pension in sterling or euros against costs in dalasi has been comfortable, but this is not a stable-price economy: the Central Bank's policy rate is 14%, held at the meeting of 20 August 2026, and inflation was 7.0% in July 2026 after 7.6% for 2025 as a whole. Our previous "7.0% in April 2026" was misdated.
  • Test a wet season first. Almost everyone visits between November and April. The FCDO records heavy rainfall from June to October, with localised flooding and difficult unpaved roads. Living here in August is a different country.
  • Sort out succession early. A Gambian property held by a foreign owner without a will is a problem for your family, not for you — see inheritance and wills for Gambian property.

Two things this guide still cannot answer, and we would rather say so: whether you can import household effects free of duty (the Customs and Excise Act and Regulations are published, but we could not extract a personal-effects relief), and what a foreign resident needs to open a Gambian bank account (no published requirements were located).

If you are marketing property to retirees

If you are a private owner, an agent or a developer selling to people who intend to live here, the residency question comes up in every conversation. Getting it wrong is not a marketing error; it is a misrepresentation about the buyer's legal status in the country. Here is the line.

What you may say

  • That a foreign buyer can hold Gambian property, subject to the Minister's consent to any assignment of a state lease and to the restrictions set out in our guide for foreign buyers.
  • That foreigners do live in The Gambia in retirement on an annually renewed residence permit — Type A, residence only.
  • That the permit application is lodged at the Immigration Department in Banjul or at a Gambian embassy, and that the buyer should confirm current fees and requirements with the Department directly.

What you must not imply

  • That buying confers residence, or helps with it. It does not. There is no residence-by-investment route and no property exception to the permit requirement.
  • That a golden visa or retirement visa exists. None is published by any Gambian government source. Do not use either phrase.
  • That the annual cost of staying legal is known and small. The non-ECOWAS Type A fee is unpublished. Any "permit plus card, about D7,500 a year" figure — including the one this site previously implied — rests on the wrong permit row.
  • That renewal is automatic, or that refusal is unlikely. No renewal criteria, refusal grounds or appeal route are published.
  • That a spouse and children are covered by the holder's permit. Unsourced. Do not put it in writing to a buyer.
  • That there is an income threshold, or that it is low. "Evidence of financial support" is required; no amount is published anywhere we could find. Inventing a comfortable figure is the easiest way to end up with a buyer who cannot stay in the house they bought.

What to perfect before you market to this buyer

A retiree buyer is unusually document-sensitive, because they intend to be present, to renovate and eventually to pass the property on. Before listing: get the title position straight (how to verify land title), understand that an undeveloped state-leasehold plot may not be lawfully assignable until it is built on, be ready for the seller's capital gains tax to fall due before registration, and be honest that estate agency in The Gambia has no licensing regime — see estate agent regulation. Money should be paid into a lawyer's client account, and a buyer should insist on it, because no Gambian rule compels it.

Questions people actually ask

Does buying property in The Gambia give me residency?

No. There is no residence-by-investment route, no golden visa and no retirement visa. A registered deed gives you a property right and nothing else. Residence is a separate, annually renewed permit from the Gambia Immigration Department.

Which permit does a retiree need?

Type A, described as being for residence only and issued to retirees and students (FactCheck Gambia, 27 August 2024). Types B and C are work permits: B for residence and employment, C for unskilled workers.

What does a Type A permit cost?

We cannot tell you, and we would rather say so than estimate. The only traceable January 2024 fee schedule contains no Type A row, and the Immigration Department published no fee schedule we could reach on 31 August 2026. Ask the Department directly and get the figure in writing before you budget.

Do Americans need a visa?

Yes. The US State Department states, as at 29 January 2026, that a visa is required and can be obtained before travel or on arrival. On arrival it costs US$100–105 in cash, and a US$20 security fee is charged on entry and again on departure.

How long can a British visitor stay without a permit?

Twenty-eight days on arrival, plus two extensions of 28 days from the Immigration Office in Banjul or the tourist police stations in the Tourism Development Area — 84 days in all. Longer stays need a residence permit (FCDO, 7 July 2026).

Is the rule the same for all EU nationals?

No. A Dutch passport holder needs no visa for a stay of 90 days or less (Netherlands Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 20 May 2026). A French national is issued a free visa on arrival valid for 15 days, renewable. Check your own foreign ministry rather than any general EU rule.

What happens if I overstay?

A fine of US$15 per month, payable in cash when you leave, according to the US State Department (29 January 2026).

Can I get permanent residence after five years?

We could not establish that a Gambian permanent-residence status exists at all, and we have deleted our previous five-to-ten-year claim. Naturalisation requires 15 years of continuous ordinary residence and renunciation of your other citizenship (Constitution 1996 revised 2018, section 12); the spouse of a Gambian citizen may apply to register after 7 years (section 11).

Will my UK State Pension keep rising if I move?

No. Annual uprating applies only in the European Economic Area, Gibraltar, Switzerland and countries with a qualifying UK social security agreement. The Gambia is not among them, so the pension is frozen at the rate first paid, and it rises again only if you return to live in the UK (GOV.UK, 6 November 2024).

Is my foreign pension taxed in The Gambia?

Not established. The Income and Value Added Tax Act 2012 governs, but its text is not published by the Gambia Revenue Authority or the Ministry of Justice, so we can state neither a residency day count nor a pension rule. The GRA confirms only that residents are liable on foreign employment income. Get your position in writing from the GRA.

Is there a UK–Gambia double taxation treaty?

Yes. The 1980 Gambia–UK Double Taxation Convention is in force, effective from 5 July 1982, applying from 1 January 1980 in The Gambia and 6 April 1980 in the UK for income tax and capital gains.

Can I drive on my UK licence?

For up to three months. After that you need a Gambian licence, applied for at a police station with proof of residency, your valid UK licence and a copy of your passport (FCDO, 7 July 2026).

Does my permit cover my spouse and children?

We do not know. No source we could trace says so, and we have removed the claim that it does. Ask the Immigration Department and get the answer in writing before you rely on it.

What will I pay when the property is eventually sold?

Capital gains tax, paid by the seller: for an individual the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration; for a company, partnership or trustee the higher of 25% of the gain or 10% of the consideration. It is payable before the deed can be registered, with a 15-day return deadline.

Pay by mobile money or card, or by bank transfer. Online payments are processed by Waychit, a Gambian payment aggregator that works through payment partners licensed by the Central Bank of The Gambia. MyKunda never sees your card or wallet details.

Bank transfers clear once the banks release them. MyKunda only collects listing and service fees — we never take deposits, down payments or purchase money. Those belong in your lawyer’s client account — insist on that in writing, because no Gambian rule compels it.
