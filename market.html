How the index is built

—

Every listing carries a price log: listed, every price change, and the closing price when it goes to sold or let. On the 1st of each month a scheduled job closes the month that ended and writes one row per segment to market_snapshots — median price, median price per m², index with the first tracked month at 100, plus month-on-month and year-on-year change. The running month is refreshed nightly, so this screen is never more than a day behind.

The index is not only our own stock. It pools MyKunda listings and closed deals with the Gambian portals, agents' own lists, notary and registry figures and manual observations — every source carrying a weight between 0 and 1, so a closed deal we witnessed counts fully and a stale international aggregator barely nudges the line. Medians are weighted medians. The same property listed on four portals is fingerprinted and counted once, at the trust of the best source that has it. Who feeds what, and whether last night's harvest worked, is on Sources.

Segments are cut seven ways at once — whole market, land versus built, land by area, built by area, region and property type — each split into sale and rent. A segment with fewer than five listings in a month is marked thin and its median is pooled over three months instead of being published on a handful of plots; that is the honest read for places like Kartong or Barra. Land is measured on plot m², built property on floor m².

Index, MoM and YoY are measured on price per m², never on the raw median: one extra villa entering a small area would otherwise look like a 40% boom. The whole-market line is a sample-weighted composite of the land and built indices, so a quiet month for plots can't tilt it. Medians in absolute money are shown alongside for context. Prices are stored in USD and converted to dalasi at the Central Bank rate for display only.

Real divides the index by the national CPI, so it shows what property did against everything else getting more expensive — in a market running 6%+ inflation, a nominal rise of 5% is a fall. The dashed line is the GBoS housing CPI on the same base, and the strip under the chart carries the rates that usually explain a bend.

Pay by mobile money or card, or by bank transfer. Online payments are processed by Waychit, a Gambian payment aggregator that works through payment partners licensed by the Central Bank of The Gambia. MyKunda never sees your card or wallet details.

Bank transfers clear once the banks release them. MyKunda only collects listing and service fees — we never take deposits, down payments or purchase money. Those belong in your lawyer’s client account — insist on that in writing, because no Gambian rule compels it.
