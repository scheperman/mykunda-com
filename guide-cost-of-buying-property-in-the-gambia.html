The real cost of buying property in The Gambia

MK MyKunda Updated 15 August 2026 8 min read

The asking price is only part of the picture. Here's what else to budget for when you buy a home or plot in The Gambia, so there are no surprises at completion.

In short

  • Budget 8–12% on top of the asking price for transfer tax, stamp duty, legal work, survey, registration and FX.
  • On a worked €80,000 villa the realistic total is €91,000–€93,000, including €4,000 transfer tax at 5% and €1,200 stamp duty at about 1.5%.
  • Once you own it, rental income is taxed at 8% residential or 15% commercial on gross rent, capital gains at the higher of 15% of the net gain or 5% of the selling price, and company-held property at 27% corporation tax.
  • MyKunda sellers pay a flat fee rather than a percentage commission, so the listing cost is identical whether a property sells for $50,000 or $500,000.

The transfer tax is the big one. It is calculated on the value stated in the deed of assignment or sale agreement, and the authorities may commission their own valuation if the declared price looks below market — which is one of several reasons not to under-declare. Exact rates change, and are applied case by case, so always get an itemised quote and a completion statement from your lawyer before the final payment.

A worked example

Take a €80,000 villa — roughly D6.8 million at the Central Bank's August 2026 valuation rate of about D85.5 to the euro (D72.6 to the dollar, D96.8 to the pound). A realistic budget looks like this:

Purchase price€80,000

As a rule of thumb: budget 8 – 12% on top of the asking price, and treat anything left over as a cushion rather than a saving. Currency movement alone can eat a chunk of it between agreement and completion.

Note that rental income tax is charged on gross rent, not profit — so a holiday let with high running costs is taxed on the top line regardless. There is no general annual property tax, but Area Councils levy rates on developed property and unpaid rates follow the property to its new owner. Since October 2025 the Gambia Revenue Authority has been rolling out a Rental Compliance System, so letting income that once went unnoticed increasingly does not.

Why a flat agent fee matters

Traditional agents often charge a percentage commission, which grows with the price of your property. On MyKunda, sellers pay a flat fee instead — so the cost of listing or full service is the same whether a property sells for $50,000 or $500,000. For buyers, that keeps the market cleaner and prices more honest.

A flat fee aligns everyone's incentives around getting the deal done well — not inflating the price to grow a commission.

Costs for diaspora buyers

If you're buying from abroad, budget also for international transfer fees and, optionally, a managed service so an established local agency handles viewings, documents and registration on your behalf. Many buyers find the managed route saves far more in avoided mistakes than it costs.

Ongoing costs

  • Property maintenance — especially for coastal properties exposed to salt air;
  • Security / caretaker — common for second homes and holiday lets;
  • Utilities and service charges — where applicable in gated estates.

Rates are indicative, applied case by case, and change — your lawyer's completion statement is the number that counts.
