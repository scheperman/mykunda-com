The real cost of buying property in The Gambia

MK MyKunda Updated 31 August 2026 16 min read

The asking price is only part of the picture — but most of the figures circulating online for the rest of it are wrong. This guide sets out what Gambian law and Gambian official sources actually publish about the cost of buying, owning and selling property here, what they do not publish, and where the widely quoted "5% transfer tax" comes from.

In short

  • There is no buyer-paid transfer tax in Gambian law. The duty on a purchase is stamp duty at a flat 2% of the amount in the deed, and the 2019 Order names the buyer as the person primarily liable.
  • The "5%" buyers are commonly quoted is the seller's capital gains tax floor — an individual seller pays the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration. It is the seller's tax, and whether it lands on you is a matter of negotiation, not law.
  • On the published figures a buyer's own costs on an €80,000 purchase come to roughly 4.5–5.5% of the price, before the cost of moving the money and before any contingency. If you also absorb the seller's capital gains tax, budget closer to 10%.
  • VAT at 15% is added by any VAT-registered lawyer, agency or contractor, and is missing from most cost estimates. A mortgage carries its own 0.5% stamp duty.
  • Deeds are registered at the Registrar General's Deeds Registry, not at the Lands Office. Assigning a state lease needs the Minister's written consent — measured at 60 days and GMD 750 in 2019 — and consent is refused where the plot has not been developed.
  • It is wrong to say there is no annual property tax: Area Council rates and, on a state lease, annual ground rent both recur. No current schedule for either is published.
  • MyKunda sellers pay a flat fee rather than a percentage commission, so the listing cost is identical whether a property sells for $50,000 or $500,000.

Two things follow from that table. First, the duty is charged on the amount stated in the instrument, so the deed's figure is what matters. The Stamp Act's assessment machinery lets the Commissioners require evidence and refuse to proceed without it, and under section 29 no deed may be registered unless duly stamped; section 17 makes an unstamped instrument inadmissible in evidence. Under-declaring is not a saving — it is an unregistrable deed and a weaker record if you are ever in court. It also depresses the acquisition cost you can later deduct when you sell.

Second, the GMD figures above are 2019 World Bank measurements, not a current fee schedule. No Gambian body publishes a current tariff for consent, survey or deed registration. Expect them to have risen; ask your lawyer for the figures the offices are actually charging on the day.

The "5%" you will be quoted — and what it really is

Ask around and you will be told that buying property in The Gambia carries a transfer tax of about 5%, sometimes "five to ten per cent". We could find no Gambian statute that imposes any such buyer-paid tax, and the property-agency pages that assert it cite no legislation at all. What the law and the Gambia Revenue Authority publish is this:

  • Stamp duty on an assignment or conveyance is 2%, and the buyer is primarily liable. That is the Stamp Act as amended by Legal Notice No. 18 of 2019.
  • Capital gains tax is charged on the person disposing of the asset — the seller. For an individual it is the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration. For a company, partnership or trustee it is the higher of 25% of the gain or 10% of the consideration.

So the famous 5% is almost certainly the seller's capital gains tax floor, described to buyers as though it were theirs. There is a second reason the number sticks. The World Bank's Doing Business 2020 profile for The Gambia records a single combined procedure — "Payment of Capital Gains Tax and Stamp Duty … 5% of the purchase price (Stamp Duty)" — on data collected in May 2019, four months before the September 2019 Order, and that entry aggregates the seller's capital gains tax with the duty. The World Bank's 2017 land governance assessment likewise reported "a fixed stamp duty cost of 5% payable by the buyer". Both pre-date the current schedule.

If a seller or an agent asks you for 5% of the price at completion and calls it transfer tax, ask which instrument it is charged under. On the published law it is either the seller's capital gains tax, in which case it is negotiable, or it is stamp duty, in which case it is 2%.

Capital gains tax matters to a buyer for a second reason: it is payable before the deed can be registered. A seller who cannot or will not settle it can strand your registration. Make evidence of the seller's capital gains tax payment a condition of your final payment, and see our guide to verifying land title in The Gambia for the rest of the completion checklist.

What we could not verify

We could not trace the "5–10% transfer tax" to any Gambian statute, gazette or Gambia Revenue Authority publication. We also could not obtain a current, published fee tariff for ministerial consent, survey plans or deed registration; the amounts above are World Bank measurements from May 2019. Ask the Gambia Revenue Authority or your lawyer to confirm in writing what rate has been assessed on your deed before you pay.

Then hold a separate contingency — the three GMD fees are seven years old, the cost of moving the money is not published, and currency movement between agreement and completion is real. The dalasi fell 11.53% against the euro over the year to February 2026 on the Central Bank's own figures.

For comparison, the World Bank measured the whole Banjul transaction — both sides, all six procedures — at 7.8% of property value over about 73 days. That is a 2019 measurement, it includes the seller's capital gains tax, and it is the best independent yardstick available. It is not a current fee schedule and no one publishes an updated one.

Consent, registration and how long it takes

Two institutional points are worth more to a buyer than any fee estimate.

Deeds are registered at the Registrar General's Deeds Registry, in the Attorney General's Chambers and Ministry of Justice, under the Land (Registration of Deeds) Act, Cap. 57:01. The Ministry of Lands issues leases and grants consents; the Registrar General registers deeds. Anyone who tells you registration happens at the "Lands Office" is describing the wrong building. Note too that this is a deeds register indexed by the names of the parties, not a title register indexed by parcel — registration gives priority, not title. Our guide to verifying land title covers what a search can and cannot tell you.

Assigning a state lease requires the Minister's written consent. Regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 provides that a lessee shall not subdivide, convey, assign or otherwise alienate the premises without the Minister's written consent first obtained, "and such consent shall not be granted if the lessee has not developed the demised premises in accordance with the covenants in that behalf". Regulation 22(k) requires the same consent to mortgage or charge the land.

The consequence catches out buyers of bare land: an undeveloped state-leasehold plot may not be lawfully assignable, and may not be mortgageable, until it is built on. The World Bank measured the consent application at 60 days and GMD 750 in 2019 — the single longest step in the process, and the one most often left out of a completion timetable. Budget time for it, and have your lawyer confirm the plot's development status before you pay a deposit. See freehold, leasehold and customary land explained for what you are actually buying.

VAT, and the costs it lands on

VAT in The Gambia is 15%, with compulsory registration once a supplier's taxable supplies reach D2,000,000. It is missing from almost every published estimate of Gambian buying costs, and it lands on real items:

  • Legal fees from a VAT-registered firm;
  • Agency and management fees from a VAT-registered agency;
  • Building and renovation contracts from a VAT-registered contractor — see building your own house in The Gambia.

The Gambia Revenue Authority's VAT material exempts the rental or supply of residential property, so residential rent does not carry VAT. Ask every professional you engage whether they are VAT-registered and whether their quote is inclusive, before you accept it.

What we could not verify

Whether VAT applies to the sale of a commercial property, or to a developer's sale of a new build, is not clearly documented in any Gambian source we could find. The exemption we could source covers residential property. If you are buying commercial premises or off-plan, get the VAT treatment confirmed in writing by the Gambia Revenue Authority or a Gambian tax adviser before you sign.

If you are borrowing

A mortgage adds its own duty. Under the 2019 Order, a mortgage is stamped at 0.5% of the amount, further charges at 0.5% and a memorandum of deposit at 0.5%; a guarantee is 0.75%. The Order names the mortgagee as primarily liable, but expect a lender to recharge it to you — ask for it as a line on the facility letter.

Security over a state lease also needs the Minister's consent under regulation 22(k), and a lender's security over an undeveloped plot is weak, because the consent needed to sell on enforcement is withheld until the build covenants are met. That, more than anything about the borrower, explains why Gambian lenders are cautious about bare land. Our guide to bank mortgages in The Gambia sets out what lenders do and do not publish, and developer financing covers the instalment alternative and its risks.

A conflict to be aware of on withholding tax. The Gambia Revenue Authority's own domestic tax FAQ page still shows the older figures of 10% for residents and 15% for non-residents. Its withholding tax brochure and the Ministry of Finance's 2025 Budget Speech give the structure in the table above. We publish the brochure and Budget figures as the current position, but the two official sources disagree — confirm the rate with the Gambia Revenue Authority before you sign a building contract.

Rental income tax is charged on gross rent, not profit, so a holiday let with heavy running costs is taxed on the top line regardless. Since the 2025 Budget, government bodies, local authorities, diplomatic missions, international organisations and large taxpayers must withhold the rental tax at source. And The Point reported on 8 January 2026, on the Authority's own review of 2025, that the first phase of a Rental Compliance System was rolled out in October 2025 — a single source, but consistent with GRA training on a digital rental income tax system going back to September 2024. Letting income that once went unnoticed increasingly does not. Our guide to renting out property in The Gambia goes into the detail.

What we could not verify

No Gambian source classifies a self-catering apartment, holiday villa or short let as residential or commercial for rental income tax. The only published boundary is GRA's statement that residential property excludes hotels, guest houses, inns and boarding houses. The difference is 8% against 15% of gross rent, so it is worth a written ruling from GRA rather than an assumption.

Ongoing costs

It is commonly said that The Gambia has no annual property tax. That is wrong. There is no national property tax, but there are at least two recurring charges on a Gambian property.

Area Council rates

The Local Government Revenue Initiative records that the eight local governments are empowered by the General Rate Act 1992 to levy property rates within their jurisdictions, with valuation prescribed under the Rating Valuation Act 1987 — nominally every five years by certified valuers, though in practice some valuation rolls are more than twenty years old. That the councils levy and chase these rates is well evidenced: Kerr Fatou reported on 19 June 2026 that Brikama Area Council had approved incentives for traditional leaders — 5% of revenue mobilised for Alkalolu, 2.5% for Seyfolu — specifically to improve property rate collection. An opinion piece in The Point in July 2020 described a Brusubi Phase 1 property rate rising from D400 to D2,000.

Annual ground rent on a state lease

If you hold a state lease, you owe annual ground rent to the Department of Lands, payable before 31 January, and non-payment is a ground for forfeiture by re-entry. The Ministry of Finance's 2026 Budget Speech commits to strengthening the annual land rent payment compliance mechanism, so this is being tightened rather than forgotten. The last published basis for calculating it is Schedule V of the State Lands Regulations 1995, which sets a slab rate per 100 m² adjusted by location and intensity indices.

What we could not verify

Three things. Current amounts: no Area Council publishes a rate schedule we could obtain, and no revision of the 1995 ground rent basis has been published — we will not quote the 1995 figures as if they were current. Whether unpaid rates follow the property to a new owner: the previously published version of this guide stated that they do. We could not obtain the text of the General Rate Act 1992 from any reliable source, and no council or ministry publication says it. It is plausible, and it is the norm in comparable Commonwealth rating statutes, but we cannot source it — so put it to the Area Council in writing before completion and ask for a rates clearance, rather than relying on the seller's word. Whether rates must be cleared before a transfer can proceed is unverified for the same reason.

The running costs nobody itemises

  • Maintenance — salt air on the coast is hard on metalwork, paint and fittings, and a property left empty in the rains deteriorates faster than one lived in;
  • Security or a caretaker — standard for second homes and holiday lets, and in practice also your evidence of occupation;
  • Utilities and estate service charges — where a gated estate levies them. Get the charge, what it covers and how it can be increased in writing before you buy;
  • Insurance and standby power — NAWEC's supply deficit means many owners budget for a generator or solar.

The five-year bar most sellers have never heard of

If your plot was allocated to you by government in an approved layout area, selling it has a consequence beyond the tax. Schedule II, paragraph 4 of the State Lands Regulations 1995 bars a person who has sold, assigned or transferred allocated land from being allocated land again for five years. Paragraph 8 imposes the same five-year bar on someone whose plot was re-entered for breach, and paragraph 5 bars a person already holding more than one plot. If you were planning to sell one government plot and apply for another, read those paragraphs first.

What to perfect before you list

Most Gambian sales that collapse do so on documents, not on price. Before you list:

  • Establish what you actually hold — a lease, a certificate of occupancy, a deed of conveyance, an Alkalo transfer, an Area Council transfer, a letter of allocation. They are not equivalent, and only some are registrable at all;
  • Get the ministerial consent question answered early if it is a state lease. If the plot is undeveloped, consent will be withheld — that is a problem to solve before a buyer is waiting, not after;
  • Bring ground rent and council rates up to date and keep the receipts;
  • Have a proper survey plan, approved by Physical Planning, rather than a sketch;
  • If you are selling an inherited property, expect to produce a grant of probate or letters of administration and, for a Muslim estate, a Cadi Court distribution order — see inheritance and wills for Gambian property;
  • Work out your capital gains position before you agree a price, because the 5% or 10% of consideration floor applies even where you have made no gain at all.
What we could not verify

Whether the sale of a commercial property or a developer's new build attracts VAT; the capital gains treatment of a gift as opposed to a sale; the detailed conditions of the private-residence and agricultural reinvestment reliefs; and whether any Gambian body publishes a normal agency commission — it does not, and neither does any statute cap one. Take Gambian tax advice on all four before you commit.

Why a flat agent fee matters

No reliable Gambian source publishes a normal agent commission, and no statute caps one — that absence is itself the answer, and it means whatever you are quoted is a negotiation rather than a market rate. On MyKunda, sellers pay a flat fee instead, so the cost of listing or full service is the same whether a property sells for $50,000 or $500,000. For buyers, that keeps the market cleaner and prices more honest.

A flat fee aligns everyone's incentives around getting the deal done well — not inflating the price to grow a commission.

Agents in The Gambia are not licensed and there is no professional register, but they are not entirely unregulated either: they are reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2012 supervised by the Financial Intelligence Unit, and complaints can go to the Gambia Competition and Consumer Protection Commission. Our guide on estate agent regulation in The Gambia sets out what that does and does not protect.

Costs for diaspora buyers

Buying from abroad adds two costs and one useful protection.

Moving the money. There is no published figure for what a property-sized transfer to The Gambia costs. The only official pricing for the corridor is the World Bank's Remittance Prices Worldwide series, which measures small remittances — for the UK to Gambia corridor it recorded a mean of 12.06% at £120 and 10.90% at £300 in Q3 2025 — and those percentages do not carry across to an €80,000 bank transfer. Get an all-in quote, in writing, that names both the fee and the exchange rate. Money transfer operators and fintechs are barred from buying and selling foreign exchange in the domestic market under the Central Bank's Foreign Exchange Policy, so a purchase of this size moves through a licensed commercial bank. Our guide to sending money to The Gambia has the detail and the dates.

Currency movement. The dalasi is free-floating and it moves. The Central Bank reported in February 2026 that it had fallen 11.53% against the euro and 4.36% against the dollar over the preceding year. Between agreement and completion that is a real number on a real purchase — price the risk, or agree the price in one currency and stick to it.

One protection worth using. If you sign abroad, the Land (Registration of Deeds) Act gives you a twelve-month window for priority to relate back to the date of execution where the instrument was executed outside The Gambia, against seven days in Banjul and sixty days elsewhere. And the Act's definition of "instrument" extends to a contract — so a sale contract, not only a final deed, can be registered. Instalment buyers in particular should use that.

Purchase money should be paid into a Gambian lawyer's client account, and you should insist on it — but be clear that no Gambian rule compels it. There is no statutory client-account or escrow requirement for agents, and no mandatory deposit protection on off-plan sales.

Questions people actually ask

Is there a transfer tax when you buy property in The Gambia?

No Gambian statute we could find imposes a buyer-paid transfer tax. The duty on an assignment or conveyance is stamp duty at 2% of the amount, and the Stamp Act (Amendment of Schedule) Order 2019 names the assignee or transferee — the buyer — as primarily liable. The "5%" widely quoted as transfer tax is, on the published law, the seller's capital gains tax floor.

How much is stamp duty in The Gambia?

Two per cent of the amount on an assignment, conveyance, lease agreement, tenancy agreement or underlease. Half a per cent on a mortgage, a further charge or a memorandum of deposit. Nought point seven five per cent on a guarantee. A flat D500 on a deed of gift, deed of assent, vesting deed, surrender and release or caveat. These are set by Legal Notice No. 18 of 2019, gazetted on 4 October 2019.

Who pays capital gains tax on a Gambian property sale?

The seller — the Gambia Revenue Authority charges the person who disposes of the asset. An individual pays the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration; a company, partnership or trustee pays the higher of 25% of the gain or 10% of the consideration. The return is due within 15 days of the disposal, and the tax must be paid before the deed can be registered, which makes it the buyer's completion risk as well.

Do I need the Minister's consent to buy a leasehold plot?

Yes, on a state lease. Regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 bars a lessee from assigning or otherwise alienating the premises without the Minister's written consent first obtained. The World Bank measured the consent application at 60 days and GMD 750 on data collected in May 2019 — the longest single step in a Gambian conveyance.

Can I buy an undeveloped plot on a state lease?

Treat it with care. Regulation 22(l) provides that consent shall not be granted if the lessee has not developed the premises in accordance with the covenants. So an undeveloped state-leasehold plot may not be lawfully assignable, and may not be mortgageable, until it is built on. Have a Gambian lawyer confirm the plot's status in writing before you pay a deposit.

Is VAT charged on the costs of buying?

VAT is 15%, with compulsory registration at D2,000,000 of taxable supplies. A VAT-registered lawyer, agency or building contractor adds it to their fee, and most published cost estimates leave it out. Residential rent is exempt. Whether VAT applies to the sale of a commercial property or a developer's new build is not documented in any source we could find.

How long does a property transfer take, and what does the whole thing cost?

The World Bank's Doing Business 2020 profile measured a Banjul transfer at 6 procedures, about 73 days and 7.8% of property value, on data collected in May 2019. That figure covers both sides of the transaction and includes the seller's capital gains tax. It is a 2019 measurement, not a current fee schedule, and no one publishes an updated one.

Is there an annual property tax in The Gambia?

There is no national property tax, but there are at least two recurring charges. Area Councils levy an annual rate on property under the General Rate Act 1992, with valuation under the Rating Valuation Act 1987. A state lease carries annual ground rent to the Department of Lands, payable before 31 January, with forfeiture by re-entry for non-payment. No current schedule for either is published.

Do unpaid council rates pass to a new owner?

We could not verify it. The text of the General Rate Act 1992 is not obtainable from any reliable source, and no council or ministry publication states it. It is plausible and it is the norm in comparable Commonwealth rating statutes, but we will not publish it as fact. Ask the Area Council for a written rates position before completion.

What is the withholding tax on a building contract?

The Gambia Revenue Authority's withholding tax brochure and the Ministry of Finance's 2025 Budget Speech give 10% for non-resident contractors and suppliers, 5% for resident contractors on government public works, and 8% for other resident suppliers, consultants and contractors. The Authority's own FAQ page still shows older figures of 10% resident and 15% non-resident. Confirm the rate with GRA before you sign.

What will it cost me to sell?

Capital gains tax is the main one — the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration for an individual, or 25% and 10% for a company, partnership or trustee. Beyond that, expect to bring ground rent and council rates up to date, to obtain ministerial consent on a state lease, and to pay whatever commission you have agreed. Stamp duty is the buyer's liability under the 2019 Order; do not concede it without getting something for it.

Does selling stop me being allocated government land again?

For five years, yes. Schedule II, paragraph 4 of the State Lands Regulations 1995 bars a person who has sold, assigned or transferred allocated land from being allocated land again for five years. Paragraph 8 applies the same five-year bar where a plot was re-entered for breach, and paragraph 5 bars anyone already holding more than one plot.

What is a normal estate agent commission in The Gambia?

No reliable Gambian source publishes one and no statute caps one. Figures circulating on property blogs have no sourcing behind them. Agree the fee in writing as a figure before you list, and be aware that agents are not licensed in The Gambia, although they are reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2012 supervised by the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Should I declare a lower price on the deed to save duty?

No. Stamp duty is charged on the amount stated in the instrument, and the Stamp Act's assessment machinery lets the Commissioners call for evidence and refuse to proceed without it. An unstamped instrument is inadmissible in evidence under section 17, and section 29 provides that no deed may be registered unless duly stamped. A low declared price also reduces the acquisition cost you can deduct when you come to sell, raising your own capital gains tax then.
