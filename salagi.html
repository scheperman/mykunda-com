The vibe

Salagi is not an old village. It is a residential layout the state carved out of Sukuta's land and allocated in plots, in two phases the Ministry of Lands calls Salagi Layout Proper and Salagi Layout Extension. Government accounts date the acquisition variously to the 1990s or to 2001, and the allocation of plots to 2005. What was farmland is now a dense grid of streets and compounds between the Sukuta traffic light and the northern edge of Salagi Forest Park, a couple of kilometres south-east of the Brusubi turntable along the AU Summit Highway.

That origin is the whole story of the place. The layout was planned with public reserves — school, nursery, cemetery, community centre, mosques, market, police and fire ground — and by the government's own account most of those reserves were never developed, which is how they came to be built on. It is a real, largely built-out address with real houses on it, and it is also an address where you must read the paperwork rather than the wall.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller. We have no rent listing we can verify here, so we quote no rental yield for this area either — a yield built on a rent we had to guess would only look like knowledge.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every verified sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.

What has happened to land at Salagi

Salagi has had two rounds of government demolition. In June 2020 the Department of Physical Planning demolished built and part-built homes; residents clashed with riot police, and the government defended the action under the State Lands Act, saying Kombo North, South and Central were designated State lands in the 1990s. Residents publicly disputed the government's claim that they had been compensated, and several said the plots they had been allocated already belonged to someone else and were already being built on.

In March 2025 it happened again. Notices dated 25 March declared structures unsafe and gave until 1 April; demolition followed on 28 March. At least 17 families were left homeless. The government's stated reason was encroachment on allocated plots and on the layout's undeveloped public reserves. Several of those demolished said they held official documents and planning approval to build. A second phase was announced, aimed at encroachments on allocated plots and on blocked access roads.

Underneath both rounds sits an unresolved question of tenure: Sukuta kabilos maintain that Salagi is customary Sukuta land that cannot be allocated without the community's consent, and a residents' association has accused the ministries of reclassifying and disposing of reserve land. Separately, part of the adjoining Salagi Forest Park was allocated to a private company in 2024, which the village development committee and the area's National Assembly Member have publicly opposed.

None of this makes Salagi unbuyable. It does mean that at Salagi the document is the asset. Ask for the allocation letter or lease from the Department of Lands and Surveys in the seller's name, check the plot against the layout plan rather than against the fence, establish whether it sits on a plot or on a reserve, and take independent legal advice before any money moves. Our guide to verifying land title sets out the searches; we can also run an ownership check for you.

Getting there and what is on the ground

Access is by the tarred AU Summit Highway, which runs from the Brusubi turntable south-east towards the airport junction; the layout lies roughly 2.5 km from the turntable, and about 0.6 km off the highway from the Sukuta traffic light. Internal residential streets are, on the evidence of OpenStreetMap's survey, largely untarred sand and laterite. Tanka Tanka, the country's only psychiatric hospital, stands on the western edge of the area, and Salagi Forest Park — 262 hectares, gazetted in 1954 — forms its southern boundary.

The layout's planned school, market, police and fire reserves were, by the government's own statement in 2025, still undeveloped. Mains water and electricity reach at least part of the layout, but we have found no coverage data we can stand behind.

What we have not measured here yet

Salagi is one of five areas we added to MyKunda in August 2026, and its page is deliberately thinner than our older ones. Rather than fill it with plausible numbers, here is what we do not know.

  • Population — no census figure is published for Salagi separately from Sukuta.
  • Rents — we have no rental listing here we can verify, so we publish no rent and no yield.
  • Schools, clinics and shops — not surveyed. The layout’s public reserves were reported undeveloped in 2025.
  • Mains water and power coverage — reported in individual listings, never in a source that covers the whole layout.
  • Whether the layout boundary touches or overlaps Salagi Forest Park. This matters for buyers and we could not resolve it.

Salagi is not recorded as a settlement in GeoNames or OpenStreetMap — only the forest park it is named after is. The pin above is the Paradise View estate inside the layout, accurate to roughly 1.5 km for the layout as a whole.
