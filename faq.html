FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about buying, selling and investing in property in The Gambia — in plain English.

Buying property

Can foreigners buy property in The Gambia?
Yes — there are no restrictions on foreign ownership of Gambian real estate, and you only need a valid passport. Note what you are usually buying, though: on the built-up coast most registered land is held on a 99-year state lease rather than freehold, so a sale is typically a transfer of the remaining lease, which needs consent from the Ministry of Lands. We strongly recommend having a local lawyer verify the title — and the remaining lease term — before any money moves.
What does "verified title" mean on MyKunda?
A listing with a Verified Title badge means the ownership documents have been reviewed by a qualified legal professional — confirming the seller has legitimate authority to sell. It's not a government guarantee, but it's the strongest practical check available and significantly reduces your risk of title disputes.
What are the typical buying costs beyond the purchase price?
Budget for roughly 8–12% on top of the purchase price: legal fees (~2–3%), stamp duty (~5%), survey fees, and agent commission (if applicable). MyKunda's Basic plan is free for sellers; buyers pay no platform fees.
How long does a purchase take?
Honestly: nobody in The Gambia can promise you a date. It depends on the documents, on the Lands Office, on surveyors and banks, and on the seller's own family — and any of those can stall a sale that looked simple. Clean, complete title documents are the single biggest thing in your favour, so start the paperwork before you start negotiating. Treat any timeline you are given, by us or by anyone else, as an estimate rather than a commitment.
Can I buy property remotely, from abroad?
Yes — many of our buyers are diaspora Gambians or international investors. You can view properties via photos and satellite maps, communicate via WhatsApp, and appoint a local lawyer to act on your behalf. We facilitate virtual viewings and can connect you with trusted legal representatives.

Selling & listing

How much does it cost to list on MyKunda?
Our Basic plan is completely free — list your property, upload photos and manage enquiries at no cost. For more visibility and features (Verified badge, priority placement, professional photos), see our Sell page for paid plan options.
What documents do I need to list?
At minimum: a description, photos and the location (area + Google Plus Code or pin on the map). For the Verified badge, you'll need to upload ownership documents (deed, allocation letter, or survey plan) which our legal team will review.
How do I get the best price for my property?
Good photos, a clear description, a verified title and an accurate Google Plus Code location all increase buyer confidence and willingness to pay. Use our free valuation tool to get a data-driven estimate based on recent sales in your area.

Location & Google Plus Codes

What is a Google Plus Code?
A Google Plus Code is a short, globally unique location code (like F833+6R — the short form used everywhere in The Gambia) that pinpoints a spot to within ~14 metres. It works like a precise address — essential in The Gambia where street names and house numbers are often unreliable. Every MyKunda listing has one, so buyers can find the exact property on Google Maps.
How do I find the Google Plus Code for my property?
The easiest way: open plus.codes or Google Maps, navigate to your property, and the code appears. Or use MyKunda's listing wizard — drag the pin on the satellite map and the code fills in automatically.
Which areas does MyKunda cover?
We focus on The Gambia's Atlantic coast — Kololi, Cape Point, Bijilo, Brufut, Sanyang, Brusubi, Bakau, Kotu, Tanji and Kartong. We're expanding inland — if your property is elsewhere in The Gambia, list it and we'll add the area.
How accurate are the prices on MyKunda?
As accurate as the evidence allows — and we show you that evidence. The Gambia has no public register of sale prices, so every figure on this site is built from asking prices we can verify, with the number of listings behind it printed next to it. Where an area has too little evidence for a figure of its own, it shows the rate of the surrounding band, clearly marked, and where we cannot defend a number at all we leave it out. Treat our figures as a measured starting point, not a quoted market: reliable Gambian market data is still being built, and building it — openly, evidence counts in view — is part of why MyKunda exists. The full method is on How we measure prices; if you know a figure to be wrong, tell us.

Safety & trust

How does MyKunda protect buyers from fraud?
Three layers: 1) Title verification by qualified legal professionals. 2) Secure document storage — ownership documents are private and access-controlled. 3) A transparent marketplace where prices, locations and property details are published openly, reducing information asymmetry. Read more on our Safe & Supported page.
Are estate agents in The Gambia licensed?
Not at present. There is no licensing regime, no professional register and no regulator for estate agents in The Gambia — an ordinary business registration is all that is required to trade. A draft Real Estate Agents Bill would introduce mandatory registration and licensing, and a separate Rent and Tenancy Bill covers some of the same ground, but neither is law yet. We explain both in our guide to estate agent regulation.
Is my personal data safe?
Yes. We follow GDPR/AVG principles: explicit consent for contact and marketing, encrypted data in transit and at rest, and a right to erasure. Your documents are stored in private, access-controlled storage. See our Privacy Policy.
