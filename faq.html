FAQ

Questions about property in The Gambia

164 answers for buyers, owners and professionals — with the evidence behind them, and a plain “we could not verify that” where the evidence runs out.

/

Foreign & diaspora buyers

Can a foreigner buy property in The Gambia?

A non-Gambian can hold and deal in Gambian property, but not without restriction, and the honest answer is more careful than the one usually given online.

What is certain: the State Lands Regulations 1995, Schedule II, paragraph 2 says in terms that “a non-Gambian shall not be allocated a plot of land.” That governs government allocation of state plots in approved layout areas — it does not, on its face, stop a non-Gambian taking an assignment of an existing lease from a private holder, which is the route foreign buyers actually use. Every such assignment needs the Minister's written consent.

Upcountry, the Lands (Regions) Act caps a non-indigene at 50 years and voids any alienation made without the required consent. “Non-indigene” is defined by descent, so some Gambian citizens are caught by it too.

What we cannot confirm: some sources, including the US State Department's 2018 Investment Climate Statement, report that non-Gambians may hold residential land only for up to 21 years, only outside 1.5 km of the high water mark, and only up to 2,500 m². We have not traced that rule to any published Gambian statute or regulation and it is not in the 1995 Regulations, so we do not state it as law — but on the coast it would matter enormously. Have a Gambian lawyer confirm the position in writing before you commit.

Do I need to live in The Gambia to own property there?

No. Residence and ownership are separate. You do not need a residence permit to hold property, and holding property does not give you one.

Does buying property give me residency or a visa?

No. There is no golden visa, no investor visa and no retirement visa in The Gambia, and buying a house confers no right to live in it. You still need the right entry stamp or a residence permit like anyone else. Residency and permits →

Can I buy without ever visiting The Gambia?

Yes, and many diaspora buyers do — through a power of attorney given to a Gambian lawyer, properly notarised and legalised where you sign it, and then registered here. Naming the instrument matters: without a valid, registered power of attorney your representative cannot sign the transfer.

One provision helps you specifically: under the Land (Registration of Deeds) Act a deed executed outside The Gambia keeps its priority if it is registered within twelve months, against seven days in Banjul and sixty days elsewhere.

Buying unseen still means you never inspect the plot. Commission an independent site visit — that is exactly what our Full Ownership Check is for.

I am a Gambian living abroad. Am I treated as a local?

For state land on the coast, your citizenship is what counts and you are treated as Gambian. Upcountry it can be different: the Lands (Regions) Act distinguishes indigenes from non-indigenes by descent, not by nationality, so a Gambian citizen buying in a district their family does not come from may still face the 50-year cap and the consent requirements.

Can I buy through a company instead of in my own name?

You can, but understand the exit cost before you do. Capital gains tax on a later sale is charged to individuals at the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration; to companies, partnerships and trustees at the higher of 25% of the gain or 10% of the consideration. That is roughly double. A company also pays corporation tax at the higher of 27% of net profit or 1% of turnover (2% if the accounts are unaudited).

Can the State take my land?

Compulsory acquisition is possible under the Land Acquisition and Compensation Act, read with section 22 of the 1997 Constitution, which allows it only on stated conditions and with compensation. The protection does not depend on your nationality. Customary land is compensated for the loss of usufructuary rights plus improvements rather than as full ownership.

Title, tenure and land

What am I actually buying — freehold, leasehold or customary land?

On the built-up coast, almost always a 99-year state lease, or a “deemed lease” that has not yet been formalised. There are four things a Gambian seller may hold:

  • Freehold — a closed, pre-1945 class. New freeholds cannot be created without the legislature's consent and surviving grants are essentially Banjul-only. A document offered as a new coastal freehold is a warning sign, not a prize.
  • State leasehold — granted by the State and signed by the Minister; 99 years for residential use, shorter for other uses.
  • Deemed leasehold — where a customary occupier in a designated area is deemed by the State Lands Act to be a lessee for 99 years, often evidenced only by a certificate of occupancy. This is the real status of a great deal of Kombo land.
  • Customary tenure in the Regions, administered through District Authorities and Alkalolu.

Full guide: the four kinds of title →

How many years are left on the lease, and does it matter?

It matters more than almost anything else and it is the question buyers most often forget to ask. A 99-year lease granted in 1998 has about seventy years to run; one granted in 1955 has thirty. Ask for the lease itself and read the commencement date — not the date it was assigned to the current holder.

What happens when the 99 years run out?

The specimen lease in Schedule III of the State Lands Regulations 1995 says the Minister may grant a further 99-year term, “provided there shall be no subsisting breach”. Renewal is therefore discretionary, not a right, although researchers report that in practice it is close to automatic. Nobody should tell you it is guaranteed.

Do I need the Ministry of Lands’ consent to buy?

Yes, for a state lease. Regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 says the lessee “shall not subdivide, convey, assign or otherwise alienate the premises … without the consent of the Minister in writing first obtained”. The World Bank measured that step at 60 days and GMD 750 in 2019 — the longest single step in a Gambian conveyance.

I want to buy a bare plot and sell it on later. Is that a problem?

It can be a serious one, and it is the single most useful thing on this page. The same regulation 22(l) adds that consent “shall not be granted if the lessee has not developed the demised premises in accordance with the covenants in that behalf.”

In plain terms: an undeveloped state-leasehold plot may not be lawfully assignable, and may not be mortgageable, until it is built on. If your plan is to buy land and flip it, get a lawyer to tell you in writing how that rule applies to the specific plot before you pay anything.

Where is a Gambian title actually searched?

At the Deeds Registry of the Registrar General's Department, which sits in the Attorney General's Chambers / Ministry of Justice — not at the Ministry of Lands, which issues leases and consents but does not register deeds. There is no public online land register; any website presenting itself as one is not official.

What does a title search actually prove?

Less than most people assume, and knowing the limits is the point.

  • It is a deeds register, not a title register. Registration gives your deed priority over later ones; it does not certify that the seller owns anything.
  • It is indexed by the names of the parties, not by parcel. A search is a name search, so you need the right names.
  • Only surveyed, mapped land can be registered. Customary land and deemed leasehold land cannot be registered at all — so for a large share of Kombo plots the central check returns nothing, and silence is not clearance.
  • Mortgages and caveats appear. Pending litigation does not — that is a separate enquiry.
What is an Alkalo transfer, and is it title?

It is evidence of a customary allocation, not registrable title, and it is routinely forged. A Brikama magistrate observed in August 2026 that it is “increasingly common for a single plot of land to be sold to several individuals, with each buyer subsequently presenting an Alkalo transfer document as proof of ownership.” Treat it as the beginning of a chain to be verified, never as the end of one.

What is a certificate of occupancy?

The document most often held by someone whose customary occupation was converted to a deemed lease when their area was designated as state land. It is real evidence of occupation, but it is not a registered lease and it cannot be searched at the Deeds Registry. Converting it into a formal lease is the step that makes land properly saleable.

What if the same plot has been sold to more than one person?

Double-selling is documented and prosecuted. The Minister for Lands told The Point in July 2025 that more than D500 million had been lost to land fraud and called the sector “a controlled mess” — a ministerial estimate rather than an audited figure, but the pattern behind it is real. The defences are a proper name search, an independent site visit that speaks to neighbours and the Alkalo, and paying nothing until consent and registration are in hand.

How big is a standard plot in The Gambia?

There is no reliable published standard, and anyone quoting one confidently is repeating a convention rather than a rule. The only statutory framework is Schedule IV of the State Lands Regulations 1995, whose residential bands run to 300 m², 301–500 m², 501–750 m² and up to 1,000 m². A dimension that does appear in court evidence is 20 m × 25 m, which is 500 m². Measure the plot; do not assume it.

Do I need a fresh survey?

Yes. A survey plan is a formal step in a Gambian transfer, not optional diligence, and an old plan does not describe what is on the ground today. A hand-drawn sketch is not a survey plan; nor is an unapproved sketch. How to verify title →

How much land in The Gambia is disputed?

Two official figures circulate and they do not agree: the Permanent Secretary at the Ministry of Lands put land-related matters at about 75% of court cases in May 2026; the Minister said 67% later the same month. No judicial statistics corroborate either. Both are assertions by officials — but they point the same way.

Costs, tax and money

What does buying actually cost on top of the price?

The figure most often repeated online — a 5% “transfer tax” — is wrong, and we used to repeat it ourselves. Correcting it is worth more than any other number on this site.

  • Stamp duty: a flat 2% of the amount, paid by the buyer. Stamp Act (Amendment of Schedule) Order 2019, Legal Notice No. 18 of 2019: “Assignment/Conveyance — 2% of amount — the assignee or transferee.”
  • Legal fees — around 2–3% of price on the World Bank's 2019 case study, plus 15% VAT if the firm is VAT-registered.
  • Ministerial consent — GMD 750, and about 60 days.
  • Survey plan — GMD 2,750 on the same 2019 measurement.
  • Registration — GMD 1,500 and about five days.

The World Bank measured the whole transfer at six procedures, about 73 days and 7.8% of property value — but that was data collected in May 2019 and it lumped the seller's capital gains tax in with the duty. Full cost guide →

So what is the “5%” everyone quotes?

Almost always the seller's capital gains tax floor. CGT for an individual is the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration — and because it has to be paid before the deed can be registered, buyers meet it at completion and often end up being asked to fund it. It is not a buyer's tax. If a seller wants you to pay it, that is a price negotiation, not a legal obligation.

Is there a transfer tax in The Gambia?

No Gambian statute imposes a buyer-paid transfer tax. Where you see one quoted at 5–10%, it is either the seller's capital gains tax described wrongly, or it is unsourced. Older World Bank publications record stamp duty at 5% because their data pre-dates the September 2019 Order and combined the two taxes into one line — worth knowing, because your lawyer may quote the older figure. Ask the Gambia Revenue Authority or your lawyer to confirm the rate applied to your deed.

Who pays the stamp duty, me or the seller?

You. The 2019 Order names “the assignee or transferee” as primarily liable on an assignment or conveyance. A deed cannot be registered unless it is duly stamped, and an unstamped instrument is inadmissible in evidence.

Is there an annual property tax?

Yes, in substance, although it is not called that. Two annual charges apply:

  • Area Council rates on developed property, levied under the General Rate Act 1992 and valued under the Rating Valuation Act 1987.
  • Ground rent on a state lease, payable to the Department of Lands before 31 January each year, with forfeiture by re-entry for non-payment.

Current amounts are not published anywhere we can reach. Ask the Area Council and the Department of Lands for the figures on the specific property, and ask for evidence that both are paid up to date.

Do unpaid rates follow the property to me?

We do not know, and we will not pretend otherwise. It is widely said, including previously on this site, but we could not obtain the text of the General Rate Act 1992 or find any council or ministry source confirming it. Treat it as a question to put to the Area Council in writing before completion, and make clearance of arrears a condition of the sale.

What tax will I pay once I own it?
  • Rental income — 8% of gross residential rent, 15% of gross commercial rent. On the gross, with no deductions, and residents and non-residents are treated the same.
  • Capital gains on sale — individuals, the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration; companies, partnerships and trustees, the higher of 25% or 10%.
  • Corporation tax, if you hold through a company — the higher of 27% of net profit or 1% of turnover (2% unaudited).
  • VAT at 15% on services from a VAT-registered supplier; residential rent is exempt.
Can I get a mortgage in The Gambia?

Rarely, and the market is genuinely tiny. No licensed Gambian bank publishes a mortgage rate, tenor or loan-to-value, and the Central Bank publishes no average lending rate — so anyone quoting you a precise range has not got it from a lender. What is published: a commercial lending rate of 22% in World Bank and IMF data for 2024 and 2025; a mortgage rate of 19.5% and a prevailing range of 9–17% in the CAHF housing finance yearbook; a maximum term of 20 years; deposits of 20–35%.

There is also a legal obstacle nobody mentions: a state lease cannot be mortgaged without the Minister's written consent, and a lender's security over an undeveloped plot is weak because the consent needed to sell on enforcement is withheld until the build covenants are met. Full mortgage guide →

Is developer instalment finance safer than it sounds?

It is a real route, and for a diaspora buyer it is often the only one — but The Gambia has no statutory regulation of off-plan sales, no developer licensing, no mandatory escrow and no buyer-deposit protection. If the developer fails, you are an unsecured creditor, and Gambian property litigation runs for years.

The one protection almost nobody uses: register your sale contract at the Deeds Registry. “Instrument” extends to a contract and “land” to any interest in real property, so registration gives your contract priority against later deeds and mortgages. Full guide →

What does it cost to send the money?

More than the advertised fee, because most of the cost is in the exchange rate. On the UK–Gambia corridor — the only Gambian corridor the World Bank measures — the range in the latest published quarter was 8.05% to 19.92% of the amount sent, with a mean of about 12% on £120 and 10.9% on £300, against a global average of 6.36%. Compare the rate, not just the fee. Full guide →

Can I take my money out again if I sell?

We cannot give you a confident yes, and we would rather say so. The Gambia runs a free-floating exchange regime with no standing capital controls, but the Central Bank Act allows the Bank to temporarily restrict the purchase, sale, holding or transfer of foreign exchange for up to twelve months, renewable once. No published rule specifically permits or restricts repatriating property sale proceeds. Money transfer operators may not deal in foreign exchange domestically, so proceeds must move through a licensed commercial bank. Plan the exit with a Gambian bank before you plan the entry.

How much cash can I bring in or take out?

Two official figures conflict. The AML/CFT Act 2012 requires a declaration of US$7,500 or more in cash or bearer instruments, both on arrival and on departure, with a fine of not less than D10,000. The Gambia Revenue Authority's customs material says US$10,000 on import. Treat US$7,500 as the safe threshold and declare.

Should I under-declare the price to save duty?

No. The Stamp Act gives the Commissioners power to assess duty, to require evidence and to refuse to proceed without it. Beyond the legal risk, the declared figure becomes your acquisition cost for capital gains tax when you sell — under-declaring now increases your tax bill later.

The buying process

What is the correct order of steps?
  1. Agree the price and the terms in writing.
  2. Your lawyer searches the Deeds Registry and checks the chain of documents.
  3. Independent verification on the ground — boundaries, occupiers, neighbours, the Alkalo.
  4. The transfer deed is drawn.
  5. A fresh survey plan is produced.
  6. The Minister's consent is applied for — allow around 60 days.
  7. Capital gains tax and stamp duty are paid.
  8. The deed is registered at the Deeds Registry.

Money should not leave your control before step 6 is resolved.

How long does a purchase take?

Nobody can promise you a date. The World Bank measured a clean Banjul transfer at about 73 days, of which 60 was ministerial consent. In practice, incomplete documents, family claims, a boundary that does not match or an unregistrable customary title can add months or years. Clean, complete papers are the single biggest thing in your favour, so start the paperwork before you start negotiating.

When is it safe to pay?

In stages, against milestones, and never into a personal account. A sensible structure is a small holding deposit into a lawyer's client account against a signed agreement; the bulk on the Minister's consent; the balance at registration.

Be clear about what escrow is here: no Gambian statute requires it, for estate agents, developers or anyone else. A lawyer's client account is a private arrangement you should insist on and document, not a protection the law gives you automatically.

Do I need my own lawyer, or can I use the seller’s?

Your own. The seller's lawyer acts for the seller. Ownership verification, a survey and legal representation are three different things, and skipping any of them to save a few hundred euros is the most expensive economy in this market.

Is my estate agent licensed?

No. Estate agency is not a licensed profession in The Gambia: no licence, no professional register, no minimum qualification and no conduct regulator. The FATF assessors put it plainly in 2022 — “no competent authority has been designated for licensing or registering and regulating the real estate sector.”

Agents are not entirely outside the law: they are reporting entities for anti-money-laundering purposes, supervised by the Financial Intelligence Unit, and consumers can complain to the Gambia Competition and Consumer Protection Commission. But nobody checks whether an agent is competent or honest before they trade. Full guide →

Where do I complain about an agent?

The Gambia Competition and Consumer Protection Commission handles consumer complaints (info@gcc.gm, +220 446 6940). Suspicious transactions go to the Financial Intelligence Unit, fraud to the police, and contractual disputes to the civil courts. There is no professional body with power to discipline an agent — the trade association that exists is voluntary and has no legal backing.

What are the warning signs I should walk away from?
  • A “freehold” being created today on the coast.
  • Pressure to pay a deposit before any search has been done.
  • Payment requested to a personal account, or in cash.
  • An Alkalo transfer offered as the only document for land in a designated state-lands area.
  • A seller who will not produce the lease itself, only a copy of a transfer.
  • A plot the seller will not let you visit, or neighbours who tell a different story.
  • A price far below the area — in a market with this much documented fraud, that is a signal, not a bargain.

Renting a home

What rights do I have as a tenant?

The Rent Act 2014, as amended by the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024, is the law in force. On the amendment as reported: rent increases are capped at 5% every three years, with a minimum interval of 36 months and three months' written notice; eviction requires 90 days' notice; and where the rent is at or below D7,000 a month it must be payable monthly rather than in large advances. It also creates a landlord habitability duty and requires consent for subletting.

One caveat we owe you: the consolidated text of the Act is not published in any official repository we can reach, so these provisions come from the National Assembly's record and press reporting of the debate. Enforcement is acknowledged to be weak.

Can my landlord ask for six months or a year up front?

Not where the rent is at or below D7,000 a month — the amendment sets that threshold for monthly payment specifically to stop large advances. Above it, the position is less clear and large advances remain common in practice.

Is my deposit protected?

No. There is no tenancy deposit scheme in The Gambia and no rule requiring an agent or landlord to hold your money separately. Evidence on the permitted deposit is conflicting — one month in some reporting, three in the bill as introduced. Get a written, signed receipt stating the amount, what it covers and when it is returned.

Does my tenancy agreement need to be stamped?

Stamp duty of 2% of the amount applies to a tenancy agreement, a lease agreement and an underlease under the 2019 Order. On a tenancy agreement the landlord or tenant is named as primarily liable — agree between you who pays before signing.

Where do I take a rent dispute?

To the rent tribunals under the Rent Act. We have not been able to establish current tribunal locations and procedure from a reliable source, so ask a Gambian lawyer or the Area Council rather than relying on anything you read online, including here.

Building

Do I need a permit to build, extend or fence?

Yes — and for the fence and the boys' quarters as well as the house. Development is controlled by the Physical Planning and Development Control Act, and building without a permit is an offence: the Planning Authority can issue a stop notice, revoke a permit and have the development removed. The statutory fine is small; the demolition is the real penalty, and it is enforced.

How long is a building permit valid?

Two years unless it is renewed. That catches out anyone building in stages, which is how most diaspora builds are financed. If you are buying a part-built house, assume the permit has lapsed until someone shows you otherwise.

Does a development permit prove I own the land?

No. The Minister for Lands said so publicly in November 2025, and demolitions have proceeded against people holding permits. A permit is planning consent; ownership is a separate question answered by the title documents.

What does it cost to build per square metre?

Honestly: nobody in The Gambia publishes a construction cost index, and we will not invent one. The Gambia Bureau of Statistics has no construction or materials index; the international cost indices do not cover the country; and the figure that circulates online traces back to a single commercial web page that does not even state which currency it means.

We publish three working tiers — around D17,000, D25,500 and D43,000 per m² — as MyKunda's own assumptions for our valuation tool, and we say so on the guide. Do not use them to set a budget or an asking price. Get quotes. Full building guide →

What does cement cost?

It moves a lot, so any figure needs a date. A 50 kg bag was around D415 on 1 January 2026 after the import tax was lifted in December 2025, D390–415 in March 2026, and about D490–500 on 20 August 2026. Supply matters as much as price — Senegalese imports rose sharply in early 2026.

Who is liable if the house turns out to be defective?

Effectively nobody, under Gambian law as it stands. The World Bank's building quality assessment records that no party is held liable for latent structural defects and there is no mandatory insurance. That makes your contract — retention held back until defects are made good, and a written defects undertaking — your only real remedy.

Do I need an environmental assessment?

Usually not for a single house. But the National Environment Agency's Class A list includes major structures within 150 metres of the high water mark, which is directly relevant on this coast. If you are building close to the beach, ask the NEA before you design, not after.

Choosing an area

Is coastal erosion a real risk where I am buying?

Yes, and it is the risk least often disclosed. The World Bank's Beyond the Tide study of February 2026 records erosion of up to 4.7 metres a year along the tourism corridor, puts the Kotu Stream to Senegambia stretch at high economic risk from 2040 and Fajara to Kotu by 2070, and notes US$2.1 billion of assets within 800 metres of the coast. A one-million-cubic-metre beach nourishment in 2003 had gone within five years.

The crucial sentence for a buyer: “No formal setback zones or planning restrictions exist yet.” There is no statutory erosion buffer to protect you. Area guide →

What is the Tourism Development Area and does it affect me?

It is the strip from Kotu to Kartong, 800 metres inland from the high water mark, held as State land under the Gambia Tourism Board's custody. If your plot is in it, allocation and consent run through the Board as well as the Ministry. Two things matter: all new TDA land applications have been suspended since 23 January 2025 pending an audit, and the 800-metre band is a tenure control, not an erosion setback — do not read it as protection from the sea.

Are there areas with known land disputes?

Several, and they are documented rather than rumoured: demolitions at Salagi in 2025; the Alkalolu of Batokunku and Tanji suspended in October 2025 over illegal layout land sales, alongside a High Court suit by forty homeowners; the Gunjur–Berending dispute running for 38 years; a protest at Brufut and Brusubi in March 2026 over reallocated land; criminal proceedings at Farato and a Salagi prosecution in August 2026. Sanyang now sits beside a US$1 billion deep-sea port, and the Ministry of Lands warned in August 2026 against unauthorised development in the port area. None of this makes an area unbuyable — it makes independent verification essential.

How reliable is the power and water?

Constrained. NAWEC was running a 26 MW deficit in April 2026 against peak demand of about 140 MW in August 2026, with roughly half of supply imported, and national electricity access at about 69% in 2024. No new water treatment plant has been built in more than seventeen years; one is under construction at Tujereng. Budget for a generator, solar, or both, and for water storage.

It is a short location code that works where street names and house numbers do not. A full ten-character code identifies a spot to about 14 metres and is globally unique. So always publish or record the full code, or the short code together with its locality.

Can I sell?

What documents prove I am entitled to sell?

The strongest is a registered deed — a lease or a conveyance registered at the Deeds Registry of the Registrar General's Department. Below that, in descending order of weight:

  • A state lease signed by the Minister, ideally with the ground rent receipts.
  • A certificate of occupancy, which evidences a deemed lease but is not itself registrable.
  • A letter of allocation, which is not a lease. The 1995 Regulations warn that if the premium is not paid within six months of the notification, no lease shall be issued at all.
  • An Alkalo transfer or Area Council transfer, which evidences a customary allocation and nothing more.

A survey plan proves boundaries, not ownership. Receipts prove payment, not title. What each document is worth →

Do I need the Minister’s consent to sell my lease?

Yes. Regulation 22(l) of the State Lands Regulations 1995 says a lessee shall not “subdivide, convey, assign or otherwise alienate the premises … without the consent of the Minister in writing first obtained”. Selling without it puts the whole transaction at risk. The World Bank measured the step at about 60 days and GMD 750 in 2019.

My plot is empty. Can I still sell it?

This is the hardest sentence on this page, and you need it before you market the plot. The same regulation continues: consent “shall not be granted if the lessee has not developed the demised premises in accordance with the covenants in that behalf.”

So an undeveloped state-leasehold plot may not be lawfully assignable until it is built on. Before you list bare land, ask a Gambian lawyer to tell you in writing whether consent will be granted on your specific plot — and if the answer is no, you need to know that before a buyer's lawyer discovers it.

My land is upcountry and customary. Can I sell it?

Only with the required consents. Under the Lands (Regions) Act, alienation without the District Authority's written consent and the Minister's approval is “null and void” — void, not merely voidable, which means the buyer gets nothing and the money is very hard to recover. A non-indigene purchaser is separately capped at 50 years.

The property is still in a deceased relative’s name. What now?

You cannot sell until the estate is regularised, and that is a layered process:

  • A grant of probate or letters of administration from the High Court — or, if nobody applies within six months of the death, the estate can pass to the Curator of Intestate Estates under the Intestate Estates Act.
  • For a Muslim estate, a Cadi Court distribution order settling the shares. The Cadi Appeals Panel has restrained disposal by sale, gift or mortgage without its approval.
  • A valuation by a qualified estate valuer.
  • Then the transfer, the Minister's consent and registration in the ordinary way.

A separate question decides everything: whether the land was individualised or is family land. Sharia inheritance rules apply to individualised land; family land is not distributed that way and may not be sellable by one branch at all. Inheritance guide →

The property is co-owned with family. Can one of us sell?

It depends on the deed and on the personal law that governs the family, and this is not a question to settle from a web page. Where a property cannot be divided without harm, Gambian Cadi authority is that it must be sold and the objecting party compelled to accept — but that is a court outcome, not something one heir can do alone. Take advice before you market it.

Can I sell from abroad?

Yes, through a power of attorney given to a Gambian lawyer, notarised and legalised where you sign it and then registered here. Buyers are rightly cautious about powers of attorney, so have yours prepared properly and be ready to evidence it.

What should I fix before I even list?
  1. Find the original title document and read it — term, commencement date, covenants.
  2. Clear any ground rent and Area Council rates, and get receipts.
  3. Commission a current survey plan and check the boundaries against your neighbours' on the ground.
  4. If you hold only a customary document, start the conversion towards a certificate of occupancy and a registrable lease. It takes time, and it is the single biggest thing you can do to the value of the plot.
  5. If the plot is undeveloped, get written advice on whether consent will be granted.
  6. If it is inherited, get the grant and any distribution order first.

Tax and the cost of selling

What tax do I pay when I sell?

Capital gains tax, and it is yours, not the buyer's. For an individual it is the higher of 15% of the gain or 5% of the consideration. For a company, partnership or trustee it is the higher of 25% or 10%. The Gambia Revenue Authority computes the gain as the selling price less the purchase price, improvements and disposal costs, and gives a return deadline of 15 days from the transaction.

When exactly is capital gains tax paid?

Before the deed can be registered. That makes it a timing issue for both sides: the buyer cannot complete registration until it is paid, so it is common for buyers to be asked to fund it. Decide who pays what at the point of agreeing the price, not at completion.

What if I sell at a loss?

You may still owe tax. Capital gains tax is the higher of the two measures, so the 5% of consideration floor applies even where there is no gain at all. Keep every receipt for what you paid and what you spent on improvements — those are what reduce the gain.

Is the stamp duty mine or the buyer’s?

The buyer's. The 2019 Order names “the assignee or transferee” as primarily liable for the 2% on an assignment or conveyance. You should not concede it as a matter of course.

Will the tax authority challenge the price I declare?

It can. The Stamp Act gives the Commissioners power to assess duty, to call for evidence and to refuse to proceed without it. Beyond that, under-declaring hurts the buyer later — the declared figure becomes their acquisition cost when they sell — so it tends to surface as a negotiation problem as well as a tax one.

Do I owe anything annually while the property is on the market?

Two things. Ground rent on a state lease, due before 31 January, with re-entry as the sanction for non-payment. And Area Council rates on developed property under the General Rate Act 1992. Arrears will come up in the buyer's checks, so clear them first. Current amounts are not published — ask the Department of Lands and your Area Council directly.

Do I pay tax on rent while I wait for a buyer?

Yes. Rental income tax is 8% of gross residential rent or 15% of gross commercial rent, with no deductions, and it applies to residents and non-residents alike. It is paid quarterly by the 15th after quarter end with an annual return by 31 March.

What do I withhold when I pay a builder before the sale?

The Gambia Revenue Authority's current withholding brochure and the 2025 Budget Speech give 10% for non-resident contractors, 5% for resident contractors on government public works and 8% for other resident suppliers, consultants and contractors. Note that GRA's own FAQ page still shows the older 10%/15% pair, so confirm the rate with GRA before you contract — we would rather show you the conflict than pick a side for you.

Is there an agent’s commission, and who pays it?

There is no statutory commission, no cap and no default rule on who pays. That absence is the answer: it is entirely negotiable, and you should agree the amount, the trigger and the payer in writing before an agent markets your property. We found no reliable published Gambian benchmark to tell you what is normal.

The selling process

What is the correct sequence for a sale?
  1. Agree price and terms in writing.
  2. Buyer's lawyer searches the Deeds Registry and reviews your documents.
  3. Transfer deed drawn.
  4. Fresh survey plan.
  5. Application for the Minister's consent — allow around 60 days.
  6. Capital gains tax and stamp duty paid.
  7. Registration at the Deeds Registry.
How long will the sale take?

About 73 days on the World Bank's 2019 measurement of a clean Banjul transfer, of which 60 days is ministerial consent. In practice, incomplete papers, a boundary mismatch or an unresolved family interest can add months. The state of your documents is the biggest variable you control.

Should I take a deposit, and how?

Take it, but not into your own hands. Agree a written escrow instruction with a law firm setting out what releases the money and what returns it. Be clear with yourself that no Gambian statute requires escrow — it is a private arrangement, and its quality depends entirely on the wording and the firm.

Do I need my own lawyer if the buyer has one?

Yes. The buyer's lawyer acts for the buyer, and the consent application, the tax computation and the deed all affect you. Separate representation is money well spent on a transaction of this size.

Do I need a new survey if I already have a plan?

Practically, yes. A fresh survey plan is treated as a formal step in the transfer, and an old plan will not reflect what is on the ground now. If your boundaries no longer agree with your neighbours', resolve that before you market — land disputes are the majority of Gambian court cases and they take years.

Do I have to disclose defects, disputes or occupiers?

There is no specific Gambian statutory property-disclosure duty that we could verify. But a misleading representation is actionable, a misdescription can unravel a sale, and a buyer's independent check will usually find an occupier or a boundary problem anyway. Disclose it, price it in, and keep the sale.

What if my buyer needs a mortgage?

Expect it to be slow and uncertain. The Gambian mortgage market is very small, no bank publishes terms, and a lease cannot be mortgaged at all without the Minister's written consent. If your plot is undeveloped, a lender's security is weak because the consent needed to sell on enforcement would be withheld — so a finance-conditional offer on bare land carries real risk of falling through. Ask for evidence of an approval in principle before you take the property off the market.

How do I avoid being defrauded by a “buyer”?
  • Never send original documents, or full scans of them, to an unverified enquirer.
  • Never accept payment into a personal account, or agree to “refund an overpayment”.
  • Insist the buyer's lawyer makes the consent application in the normal way.
  • Be wary of anyone who wants to complete unusually fast, or who resists a survey.
  • Meet viewers with someone else present, and take a note of who came.

Letting instead of selling

Is letting a good alternative while I decide?

It can be, but run the numbers honestly. Rental income tax is charged on gross rent, so a holiday let with high running costs is taxed on the top line regardless of whether it made money. The Gambian season peaks November to April — six months, not twelve — and no published Gambian series exists for rents, nightly rates or occupancy, so treat any yield you are shown, including ours, as an assumption rather than a measurement. What are my obligations as a landlord?

Under the Rent Act 2014 as amended in 2024: rent increases capped at 5% every three years with three months' written notice, 90 days' notice to evict, monthly payment where the rent is at or below D7,000, a habitability duty and consent required for subletting. The consolidated text is not published anywhere we can reach, so take advice before relying on the detail.

Will my tenant deduct tax from the rent?

Some will, and it is easy to be caught out. Since the 2025 Budget, ministries, agencies, local authorities, diplomatic missions, international organisations and large taxpayers must withhold rental tax at source — which is precisely the embassy and NGO tenant everyone wants. Agree in the tenancy who accounts for the tax.

Does a short let need a licence?

We could not establish it either way. The Gambia Tourism Board licenses tourism accommodation, but nothing published says whether a private self-catering apartment falls inside that regime. The honest advice is to ask the Board before you advertise, rather than assume. The Revenue Authority has likewise not classified self-catering lets as residential or commercial for tax.

Regulation and licensing

Do I need a licence to trade as an estate agent in The Gambia?

No. Estate agency is not a licensed profession here: there is no licence, no professional register, no minimum qualification and no conduct regulator. The FATF/GIABA mutual evaluation put it plainly in 2022 — “no competent authority has been designated for licensing or registering and regulating the real estate sector.” An ordinary business registration is all that is needed to trade.

Am I regulated for anything at all?

Yes, for anti-money-laundering purposes, and this is the part most agents miss. Estate agents are reporting entities under the AML/CFT Act 2012, supervised by the Financial Intelligence Unit. The FATF assessors noted that agents are in fact the only category of designated non-financial business that has received any supervision.

You are also bound by the Consumer Protection Act 2014, enforced by the Gambia Competition and Consumer Protection Commission — though note that the Act contains no definition of “goods” and no reference to land, and its application to property is contested. Do not rely on it either as a shield or as a sword.

What are my anti-money-laundering duties in practice?

Broadly: identify your customer, keep records, and report suspicion. The published thresholds in the Act are identification of an occasional customer above D200,000; establishing the purpose, origin and destination of cash transactions above US$10,000; source-of-wealth checks on politically exposed persons; a mandatory refusal to proceed where identity evidence is unsatisfactory; and record retention of five years for transactions above D5,000. The detail of registration and reporting practice for agents is not published — confirm your obligations directly with the FIU rather than assuming.

Where does the Real Estate Agents Bill stand?

It is a draft, and only a draft. It was prepared at the Attorney General's Chambers and validated at a national stakeholder workshop on 29 April 2026 at the NaNA Conference Hall, with World Bank support. As at 31 August 2026 it does not appear among the fifteen bills on the National Assembly's bill tracker — it has not been introduced, let alone passed or assented to. Its full text is unpublished, so nobody can tell you what it will require.

Context worth holding: the presidential election is on 5 December 2026, which compresses the legislative calendar for anything not yet introduced. Full guide →

What is the Rent and Tenancy Bill 2025?

A bill of that name is reported to exist and to have been through a Ministry of Justice validation workshop, but that rests on a single government page we could not read, and it is not on the bill tracker. We are not going to describe its contents on that basis. Watch it, but do not plan around it.

What rent law is actually in force?

The Rent Act 2014 as amended by the Rent (Amendment) Act 2024, which the National Assembly's own tracker records as assented after the Rent (Amendment) Bill 2023 passed on 21 March 2024. As reported: rent increases capped at 5% every three years with a minimum 36-month interval and three months' written notice; 90 days' notice to evict; monthly payment where the rent is at or below D7,000; a landlord habitability duty; consent required for subletting. If you manage tenancies, this is the law you are working under — not the draft bill.

Must I hold client money in a separate account?

No statutory client-account rule exists for estate agents in The Gambia. Nothing compels you to segregate, and nothing protects your client if you do not. That is a gap you can turn into an advantage: use a law firm's client account, say so in your terms, and put the escrow instruction in writing. Compare Ghana, where the Real Estate Agency Act 2020 makes a separate client account compulsory and prohibits commingling.

Is there a cap on my commission?

No cap, no statutory norm and no default rule on who pays. We found no reliable published Gambian benchmark, and that absence is itself the answer — everything is negotiable and everything should be in writing before you market.

Do I need professional indemnity insurance?

Not by law. There is no mandatory insurance for estate agents, and none for construction defects either. Carrying cover voluntarily is a real differentiator in a market where buyers have no register to check you against.

Is there a professional body I can join?

A voluntary association exists, and the FATF assessors described the sector's body as one that “does not have any legal backing” with voluntary membership. It cannot license you, discipline a competitor or give a buyer a route of redress. Treat membership as networking, not as accreditation.

How does The Gambia compare with its neighbours?

Ghana passed the Real Estate Agency Act 2020 (Act 1047), assented on 29 December 2020: a Real Estate Agency Council, mandatory licensing, a compulsory separate client account with no commingling, and penalties of 5,000–10,000 penalty units and/or five to ten years for unlicensed practice. That is the shape Gambian reform is likely to take. We have not been able to verify the position in Senegal from an official source, so we make no claim about it.

Setting up an agency

What registration do I actually need?

An ordinary business or company registration under the Companies Act 2013 and the Single Window Business Registry Act 2013, through the Registrar General at the Ministry of Justice. There is no sector-specific approval to obtain because no sector regulator exists. You will also need a Taxpayer Identification Number from the Gambia Revenue Authority.

What corporation tax will my agency pay?

The higher of 27% of net profit or 1% of turnover — 2% of turnover if your accounts are unaudited. The turnover minimum bites in a loss-making year, which is worth modelling before you take on fixed costs.

Do I need to register for VAT?

Registration is compulsory at D2,000,000 of taxable supplies, and the rate is 15%. Agency fees from a VAT-registered firm carry it. Residential rent is exempt; the treatment of residential sales is not something we could establish from a reliable source, so take advice rather than assuming.

What do I withhold when I pay contractors and consultants?

On the Gambia Revenue Authority's current withholding brochure and the 2025 Budget Speech: 10% for non-resident contractors, 5% for resident contractors on government public works, and 8% for other resident suppliers, consultants, contractors and subcontractors. GRA's own FAQ page still shows an older 10%/15% pair — the conflict is real and unresolved on their own site, so confirm with GRA before you contract.

Do I have to tell buyers I am an agent rather than the owner?

Legally, there is no specific Gambian disclosure rule we could verify. Practically, not disclosing it is a misleading-representation risk, and on MyKunda it is required — professional listings carry the agency's identity. In a market with no register, being visibly and consistently who you say you are is the whole of your reputation.

Developers and off-plan

Do I need a developer licence?

No. There is no statutory regulation of off-plan sales, no developer licensing, no mandatory escrow and no buyer-deposit protection in The Gambia. Four independent sources say so, including the CAHF housing finance yearbook and the Gambian press reporting the GCCPC's own study recommending that the sector be given a regulator.

Must I hold buyers’ instalments in trust?

No statute requires it. If you offer escrow it is a private contractual arrangement, and its value to the buyer depends entirely on how it is drafted. Offering a genuine, documented escrow through a law firm is one of the few ways to differentiate yourself in an unregulated market.

What should be in my sale contract that usually is not?

The estate, not just the unit. The clearest Gambian lesson on this came from the Dalaba Estate dispute, where buyers who had paid D2–3 million each petitioned the developer over undelivered roads, solar lighting, a playground and a shopping centre, and the chief executive replied that “purchasing involves contractual agreements, not promises.” He was right — which is precisely why infrastructure and amenities need dates and remedies in the contract, not renderings in the brochure.

Can I subdivide a leasehold site and sell plots?

Only with the Minister's written consent under regulation 22(l) — and that consent is refused where the premises have not been developed in accordance with the lease covenants. Plan the phasing around that rule, because a contract clause cannot override a Minister's refusal.

Can I mortgage the site to fund the build?

Only with the Minister's written consent under regulation 22(k). Lenders know that enforcement then needs a second consent under 22(l), which is withheld on undeveloped land — which is why security over a bare site is weak and why development finance is scarce. Note also that construction has one of the highest non-performing loan rates in the banking system, at 18.7% on the Central Bank's figures.

What planning law governs my scheme?

The Physical Planning and Development Control Act, with a Planning Board and Planning Authorities, development control, enforcement notices, stop notices, revocation of permits and an appeal to the Minister. Two practical points: a development permit is valid for two years unless renewed, and a permit is required for the fence and the boys' quarters as well as the house. Building without one is an offence and the remedy is removal of the development.

Two institutional caveats worth knowing: the National Physical Planning Board was only inaugurated on 4 March 2026 after acknowledged dysfunction, and there is an unresolved tension between the planning Act and the Local Government Act 2002 over who the planning authority actually is.

Are there coastal restrictions on my site?

Two separate regimes, often confused. The Tourism Development Area runs from Kotu to Kartong, 800 metres inland from the high water mark, on State land in the Gambia Tourism Board's custody — and all new TDA land applications have been suspended since 23 January 2025 pending an audit. Separately, the National Environment Agency's Class A list includes major structures within 150 metres of the high water mark, which triggers environmental assessment.

Neither is an erosion setback. The World Bank recorded in February 2026 that “no formal setback zones or planning restrictions exist yet” — with erosion of up to 4.7 metres a year on the tourism corridor. Design for that yourself; the law will not do it for you.

Am I liable for structural defects after handover?

Under Gambian law as assessed by the World Bank, no party is held liable for latent structural defects and there is no mandatory insurance. So the answer is: only to the extent your contract says so. Offering a written defects undertaking with a retention is genuinely unusual here, and buyers notice.

What happens if a buyer stops paying?

Whatever your contract says — forfeiture of instalments is purely contractual and unregulated, and we could not find Gambian authority on whether an aggressive forfeiture clause would be enforced. Draft it, but draft it proportionately, and expect a court to look at it if it is ever tested. Gambian property litigation commonly runs over three years.

Data protection

Am I a data controller for the enquiries I receive?

Yes. Under The Gambia's Data Protection and Privacy Act, assented on 15 July 2026, an agency that decides how and why enquirer data is processed is a controller, with duties on transparency, security, records of processing, impact assessments and breach notification — and, in some cases, an obligation to appoint a data protection officer. The Act creates criminal offences, including for concealing a breach.

Who is the data regulator?

Not a new body: the Act designates the Information Commission established under the Access to Information Act 2021, whose commissioners were sworn in on 5 August 2024. Appeals go to the High Court.

Can I market to my enquirer list?

Only on a lawful basis, and consent under the Act must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action. People have a right to object. If any of your enquirers are in the EU or the UK, the GDPR applies to you as well, because it reaches processing aimed at people there.

Can I export my leads to a CRM hosted abroad?

That is a cross-border transfer under the Act. You need to assess the protection available in the receiving country, document the assessment, and be aware that the Commission may prohibit or suspend transfers. Choose your CRM with that in mind rather than after the fact.

Per listing. Verification is a check of the documents supplied for a specific property, so it cannot be inherited by the next listing. That is deliberate — a badge that attached to a company rather than to a title would tell buyers almost nothing.

Can I list off-plan units before the permit is granted?

We would rather you did not, and you should think hard about it. Building without a permit is an offence, buyers have no statutory deposit protection, and a listing that turns out to be undeliverable damages you more than the early enquiries are worth. If you do list ahead of permission, say so plainly in the listing.

How do you handle duplicate listings of the same plot?

Seriously, because double-selling is a documented Gambian fraud rather than a hypothetical one. Every listing is reviewed before it goes live, and because each carries a Plus Code, two listings pointing at the same ground are visible to us in a way they are not on a portal that only records a town name. If you believe a plot of yours has been listed by someone else, tell us and send your documents.

What happens if one of my listings turns out to be fraudulent?

It comes down. Repeated problems end the account. We would rather lose a listing than have a buyer lose a deposit — a portal with no filter is worth nothing in this market.

Do leads come to me directly?

Yes. Enquiries and viewing requests reach you in the app and on WhatsApp, and they appear in your leads pipeline so nothing is lost between messages. We do not sit between you and your client, and we do not resell your leads.

What analytics do I get?

Views per listing with a daily history, enquiry counts, your pipeline by stage, and your median response time measured from when a lead arrives to when you first act on it. Median rather than average, and a dash rather than a zero where there is nothing to measure.

Will MyKunda hold client money for me?

No. We collect listing and service fees only — never deposits, down payments or purchase money. Client money belongs in a law firm's client account, and since no Gambian rule compels that, saying so clearly in your own terms is worth doing.

Which areas can I list in?

All 46 published areas across the seven regions, coast to Upper River. If your property sits somewhere we have not yet published an area page for, list it anyway and tell us — we add areas on evidence.

How can I pay?

Mobile money (Wave, Afrimoney, QMoney, APS Wallet), card (Visa, Mastercard) or bank transfer from any Gambian bank. Online payments are processed by Waychit, a Gambian payment aggregator operating through payment partners licensed by the Central Bank of The Gambia. Invoices are in your dashboard under Account.

What happens to my listings if I leave?

They come down and your data can be erased on request, free of charge, as the Gambian Act requires. Nothing is held hostage.
