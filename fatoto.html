The vibe

Fatoto is where the south-bank road runs out, close to the Senegalese border at the eastern tip of the country. It is a farming town with a ferry crossing, a weekly market and very little else — and land here is the cheapest in The Gambia by a clear margin. Buyers are almost exclusively families with roots in the area or people buying farmland. Anything requiring materials, contractors or reliable power should be planned around a long supply line from Basse.

At these prices the money is not the risk; the ownership is. Read freehold, leasehold and customary land explained and how to verify a land title.

Evidence behind the land rate: no local observations — regional rate. Behind the rent: no rental listings we can verify. Gambian tenants think in months; agents advertise by the year, and landlords normally ask six to twelve months up front, so both units are shown.

We have no plot listings of our own for Fatoto yet. The rate above is the provincial regional rate, and it can be wide of a specific street by a factor of two. Upcountry we publish land only: there is not enough house or rental advertising in Fatoto to put a number on either, and a number we cannot defend is worse than none.

Every figure on this page is an asking price. No one in The Gambia publishes what property actually sells for — there is no public register of sale prices — so what a seller asks is the only thing that can be measured consistently. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller.

These figures are as honest as we can make them — and still young. Reliable Gambian market data barely exists yet, for anyone; building it is one of the reasons MyKunda exists at all. So we publish what we can measure, print the evidence count next to every figure, and leave a blank rather than defend a number we cannot. A market where buyers and sellers can trust what they read has to start somewhere. Every listing and every verified sale sharpens these figures — and if you know a figure on this page to be wrong, tell us.
