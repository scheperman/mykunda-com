Method · MyKunda price index

How we measure these prices

A price index for a country with no public register of sale prices has to say exactly what it is made of. This is that statement: the sources, what each one counts for, what gets thrown away, and where the number stops being useful.

The MyKunda price index is an average asking price per square metre for each of the 41 areas we track, published in US dollars and rebuilt every month. It is assembled from listings — ours, other people's, and agents' own lists — plus the occasional real deal we can see the papers for. Nothing in it is modelled or estimated to fill a gap.

Last rebuilt 24 August 2026.

Where the observations come from

Every observation carries a weight between 0 and 1, and the area averages are weighted medians rather than plain ones — so a handful of unusual asking prices cannot drag an area up or down. The weight says how close the evidence is to a real transaction:

Kind of evidenceWeightWhy
A closed deal we witnessed1.00A price someone actually paid, with papers we saw.
A notary figure0.95Recorded by a professional, but second-hand to us.
Our own asking price0.90We know the property, the size and the title. Still an ask.
An agency's own list0.85Named agent, current stock, sizes usually reliable.
A Gambian portal0.60 – 0.65Wide coverage, uneven data — sizes and areas often loose.
An international aggregator0.40Broad stock, but frequently years out of date.

That weight is then multiplied by how confident the reader was about the row itself — a half-legible listing counts for a fraction of a clean one. The effect is deliberate: a real deal can never be outvoted by a pile of scraped guesses.

What gets left out

The same villa on four portals is one villa

Every row is fingerprinted on market, property type, area, size to the nearest 25 m² and price to within about 5%. Where fingerprints match, only the copy from the most trusted source survives; anything matching one of our own listings is dropped outright. Without this step the coast would look busier and cheaper than it is, because the same stock is re-advertised many times over.

Outliers, and anything we could not read

Prices far outside their area's distribution are excluded rather than smoothed — usually they are a currency mix-up, a whole apartment block priced as one unit, or a plot measured in hectares. Listings without a usable size are excluded too: without square metres there is no price per square metre. When a portal redesigns and our reader stops understanding it, the source drops out of that month's index rather than contributing half-read rows.

Official statistics are context, never input

Inflation, the policy rate and the dalasi exchange rate are tracked alongside the index but are never priced into it. They are used to read the index — to say whether a rise is real or currency — and to date it. They do not move an area's average.

How often it is updated

The index is rebuilt monthly and every published figure carries the month it was measured. Areas with thin evidence in a given month keep the previous reading rather than being refreshed on one or two listings — a number that jumps because three plots came onto the market is worse than no change at all. The year-on-year column compares the same month a year earlier.

What this is not

It is not a valuation

An area average cannot value a property. Title, access, water, power, boundaries and the state of the building all move a real price far more than the area does, and none of them are in this number. Use it to sanity-check an asking price and to compare areas — not to decide what a specific plot or house is worth. For that you need the papers, which is what Ownership Verification is for.

These are not transaction prices

There is no public land registry of sale prices in The Gambia, so no one — us included — can publish verified transaction data for the country. Everything here is what was asked, consistently measured. Sale prices are generally lower, by an amount that depends entirely on the seller.

It is not an official register

MyKunda is a property platform, not a statistics office. This index is our own measurement, published so it can be checked and argued with. It carries no official standing, and where a figure elsewhere on this site comes from an outside body, that body is named on the page.

Who is responsible for it

The index and the written guides are produced and checked by MyKunda, with Edwin Scheperman responsible for what is published. If a figure looks wrong to you, tell us — with the listing or the area, if you can. Corrections are made in the next monthly rebuild, or immediately if something is plainly broken. Who does what is set out on the team page.
